# research-sim

The downstream simulator of the UnFrack.It UTxO-management research. This folder holds four parts of it: the history reader, the simulator core, the baselines and the oracle.

The history reader turns a cardano-db-sync database into the simulator's inputs, for given wallets and windows. For each wallet (a stake credential) and window it writes one history file. The file holds the wallet's UTxO set at the window's start, then every event of the window in chain order: deposits, held UTxOs consumed, spends with their intents, self-reorganizations, and 30-day marks. The reader also writes a wallet's state at the snapshot block, and computes the census, near-cap and collateral quantities and the battery pools as separate commands.

The simulator core replays those histories and builds the batteries, offline: a ledger kit that assembles and checks transactions exactly, wallet states, a bridge to the treatment, the method contract every selector implements, the arms and their run plans, the replay engine, the batteries, and the synthetic shapes with their intent streams. It reads no database and no chain.

The baselines are the external selectors the treatment is compared with: `csl-lf`, `csl-ri`, `csl-lf-pure`, `csl-ri-pure`, `cjs-rrri` and `evo-lf`. Each runs at its library's defaults through an adapter that implements the method contract. A builder survey also runs Mesh and the Evolution SDK's `unfrack` option.

The oracle computes the reference a selector's fee is measured against: for one battery spend and one change policy, the least fee it finds for any input set in a single transaction, with a status that says how far that fee is proven.

A few terms recur below. The extraction is the study's sampling tool in this repository, `tools/research-corpus`; it builds its tables in a database schema named `study`. A frame is the window a wallet was sampled for: `recent` (the 365 days ending at the snapshot block) or `peak` (the busiest 73 epochs since the Mary hard fork). Every sampled wallet is in one split: exploration, which may be read at any time, or confirmation, which is read only after the study's analysis scripts are frozen on chain. The treatment is the code under test, UnFrack.It's planner and selection rule; its files are published with the study's archive, not in this repository.

The study's hypotheses are numbered H-1 to H-12. This README refers to ten of them, by these labels:

- H-1: a wallet in the tidy layout pays less fee for its later spends, and has fewer build failures, than the same wallet untidied, under the coin selectors wallets and dApps use.
- H-2: NFTs of one policy should share outputs, which the study compares with grouping by arrival transaction, by policy then arrival, byte-optimal packing and random grouping.
- H-3: a cap of 30 tokens per output is a sensible default bundle size for every holder, where the alternative is a size that follows how often a holder's tokens are needed.
- H-4: isolating each fungible asset on its own UTxO makes fungible spends smaller and less likely to fail, compared with isolation per policy and with none.
- H-5: a graded ladder of 8 pure-ADA outputs serves parallel and ordinary spends better than one change output or the current 50/15/10/10/5/5/5 split above 100 ADA; the candidates are R0, one change output; R1, that split; R2, fixed denominations on a 1-2-5 series from 5 ADA; R3, the ladder of 8; R4 and R5, ladders of 4 and 12; and R6, no reserve rule, where the planner leaves pure ADA alone.
- H-6: proposing a pure-ADA-only transaction at the acceptability thresholds (more than 8 pure-ADA UTxOs, two or more under 2 ADA, or too few collateral-grade UTxOs) keeps the wallet usable, compared with never proposing one.
- H-9 (secondary): change emitted by the layout rules keeps a wallet tidy at lower total cost than the library's default change followed by re-tidying, measured on the tidy-once arm under each change policy by the share of token UTxOs that conform at the window's end, the fee of the plan that would restore the layout, and the window's fees. The contrast it is decided on, over the spends built in both arms with the same seeds, is the tidy-once arm with layout-preserving change, costed as the wallet-controlled fees of those spends plus the fee of the plan that restores the layout at window end, against the maintained-tidy arm with the primary selector's default change, costed as the wallet-controlled fees of those spends plus the fees of every tidy plan it executes after the window start; the start plan is left out of both.
- H-10: separating tokens from pure ADA pays off most for large ADA payments.
- H-11 (secondary): over a replayed window, the choice of change strategy moves build failures and fees more than the choice of selector.
- H-12: UnFrack.It's own input selection completes more of the spends a wallet's balance allows than the library selectors do, at no greater cost for the spend and the tidying that follows it.

## Where it may run

- **Preprod**: anything, at any time, through a read-only role. All development and every test of the reader runs there.
- **Mainnet, before the final freeze**: only the `live-fire` command, after the hypotheses record is on chain, and only on the wallets an extraction live-fire run picked ("Live-fire checks" below). Every other command is refused.
- **Mainnet, after the final freeze**: only the study's own run, and only once the extraction tool's study run for the final freeze has finished. Every wallet a command reads must be in that run's sample (`study.sample`), and a confirmation-split wallet still needs `--analysis-freeze-tx`. The census is the one exception: it reads every eligible wallet of the recent frame, as the study's census of the snapshot does. Debugging after the final freeze uses the exploration split, the synthetic shapes and preprod only; before it, the live-fire checks are the only runs on mainnet wallets, and once a study run of the final freeze is recorded, the live-fire command is refused.

Every command that connects reads db-sync's `meta` table first, and on mainnet it passes the gate below before it reads any wallet. The run the gate reads fixes the snapshot and the windows. The mainnet code paths, such as the ImmutableDB byte source, are tested against preprod's own node.

`EXPLAIN ANALYZE` executes its query, so the code refuses it on mainnet in every case; plain `EXPLAIN` is allowed everywhere.

### The mainnet gate

The gate is one check in the connection (`src/history/db.mjs`, queries in `sql/gate.sql`), made after the network is read and before the connection reaches any command. A mainnet connection names its path, which the code that opens the connection sets; no command-line option can set it. There are two paths, `final-freeze` and `live-fire`, and any other mainnet connection is refused, with the reason.

**The final-freeze path.** The extraction tool's driver checks the final freeze record, the code manifest and the check log before its study run reads a wallet, and records that run in its `study` schema. The gate reads that record and does not verify the freeze again. It passes when:

- the role can read `study.run` and `study.param`;
- exactly one run's `freeze` row has the role `final-freeze`, and that run is recorded for mainnet;
- that run's `mode` row is `study`, and it has a `finished` row, which the driver deletes whenever the run starts or resumes and writes as its last step;
- its `snapshot` row is a block fixed by rule, with its id, hash and last transaction id, and its `windows` row gives both windows' blocks and transaction-id bounds;
- each `study` table this code reads (`frame`, `sample`, `franken`, `txo`, `txo_asset` and `asset`) carries that run's mark, the comment `research-corpus run <id>` that the extraction's runner writes on every table a step builds, so the samples and flags read are that run's.

Every command on the connection then uses that run's snapshot block and windows, as `--extraction-run` would give them, and checks them against the database. A `--snapshot` naming any other block is refused, and so is an `--extraction-run` naming another run.

The commands on the connection then read only wallets of that run's sample. `history` refuses a wallet named by `--wallet` or `--wallets` that is not in `study.sample`; a stake address is looked up to learn its stake-credential id, and nothing else of the wallet is read before the refusal. `--from-study` reads the sample itself. `census` and `near-cap` refuse `--all-wallets` and any wallet named outside the sample; with `--from-study`, near-cap reads the union of the random samples, and the census reads the recent frame's eligible wallets, which is its population. A role that cannot read `study.sample` reads no wallet on mainnet. A refusal names no wallet. The extraction's own refusals follow: a wallet its reconstruction check excluded, and a confirmation-split wallet without `--analysis-freeze-tx`.

**The live-fire path.** Only the `live-fire` command opens it, before the final freeze. Before its first query, the command reads the extraction run's pick of the simulator's live-fire wallets and checks it (`src/study/checklog.mjs`); the gate then reads the pick again and passes when:

- the mainnet check log's sequence and digest chain hold;
- the log has the extraction run's `live-fire-start` entry, which that tool writes only after its gate has found the hypotheses record on chain, so the hypotheses freeze is on chain;
- the run's `live-fire` end entry kept the pick (`sim_wallets: kept for the simulator checks`), and no successful `cleanup` of the run follows it;
- `sim/wallets.json` is in the run's directory and names that run, mainnet, the test value of h that the run's entries record, the snapshot block, and one to ten stake addresses;
- the pick is the one the command read;
- the role can read `study.run` and `study.param`, and no run of the final freeze is recorded there.

The grant is the pick. Every command on the connection reads the pick's snapshot block, given by hash, and no other, and takes no `--extraction-run`. It reads only the pick's wallets and no `study` table, and refuses `--from-study` and `--all-wallets`. On a test network the command applies the same restriction to its stages without the gate.

## Install

Node 24 is required (`engines` in `package.json`). From this folder:

```
npm ci
npm test
node src/cli.mjs help
```

`npm test` needs no treatment and skips the unit tests that do.

The tool has its own `package.json` and lockfile. Its runtime dependencies are `pg` (node-postgres) 8.23.0, the three baseline libraries that come from npm (`@cardano-sdk/input-selection`, `@evolution-sdk/evolution` and `@meshsdk/transaction`) and the oracle's solver `highs` 1.15.4, each pinned with its npm integrity. cardano-serialization-lib is not among them. `npm ci --ignore-scripts` installs the same tree without compiling `chacha-native`, an optional native dependency of `chacha` (which `@cardano-sdk/key-management` uses), and `chacha` then loads its JavaScript implementation; no adapter calls it.

cardano-serialization-lib (CSL) comes only through the treatment's `cslkit.mjs`, so that the reader, the treatment and every adapter share one CSL instance. This folder's lockfile holds no CSL build: the treatment directory carries its own `package.json` and lockfile, whose SHA-256 digests the pins list beside the treatment's source files, and the `csl-*` adapters refuse to run unless `cslkit.mjs` reports `CSL_VERSION` 13.2.1 (`src/methods/csl.mjs`). `--treatment <dir>` names the directory holding the treatment's files, and `--treatment-pins <file>` a list of their SHA-256 digests, one `<64 hex><two spaces><file name>` per line; `replay`, `battery` and `fidelity` use `data/treatment-pins.txt` when `--treatment-pins` is absent. Before anything is imported, every listed file's digest is compared with the file in that directory, and any difference refuses the load, naming the file. The `history` and `census` commands need both options (they have no default list, and `--treatment` alone is refused); `replay`, `battery` and `fidelity` need `--treatment`; `near-cap`, `collateral` and `battery-pools` need neither.

## The database

cardano-db-sync 13.7 with the core `tx_out` layout, `tx_out` values enabled, and multi-asset, metadata, Plutus and ledger data inserted; never `prune`, `bootstrap` or `only_utxo`. The queries read schema `public`, and the extraction's `study` tables when the role can read them (its samples with their split and reconstruction-check exclusion, its franken flags and its life table, plus, on mainnet, the run records the gate reads). Each filters on an indexed column, except the chain-wide collateral measurement and the epoch boundaries of the near-cap command, which read their tables in one pass each. Reads by transaction, reference or wallet go in batches (200 transactions, 1,000 or 2,000 output references, or 500 wallets per statement), so that no single statement runs long. Nothing is created anywhere.

Connection: `PGHOST`, `PGPORT`, `PGDATABASE`, `PGUSER`. The password comes from the file `PGPASSFILE` names (libpq's format, mode 0600). It is read when the connection opens and never printed or logged. Every session is read only.

## Transaction bytes

Every spend's intent is read from the transaction's own bytes:

- `tx_cbor` (the default on a test network): db-sync's `tx_cbor` table, kept when db-sync runs with its `tx_cbor` option on.
- `immutable` (the mainnet source): the node's ImmutableDB chunk files, read without modification, locally or over SSH with `cat`, `stat` and `dd` (`--bytes immutable --immutable-dir <dir> [--immutable-host <host>]`). Each block is found through its chunk's secondary index and checked against the index entry's CRC-32. Each transaction is reassembled from the block's body, witness-set, validity and auxiliary-data segments with their original bytes.

The on-disk format is read from `IntersectMBO/ouroboros-consensus` at commit a0e8f68afeac411fcc8262f9271438314a87ed02: `ImmutableDB/Impl/Util.hs`, `Impl/Index/Secondary.hs` and `Chunks/Internal.hs`, and the era envelope in `ouroboros-consensus-cardano`, `Cardano/Node.hs` (`encodeDiskHfcBlock`). Every ledger path below is in `IntersectMBO/cardano-ledger` at commit 391db20866113f08bc6317cb5c341304365986c6; the block layout is the Conway CDDL's (`eras/conway/impl/cddl/data/conway.cddl:8-14`).

Every transaction read from either source must hash to db-sync's transaction hash (BLAKE2b-256 of its body bytes) and have the size db-sync records, the ledger's size: the length of `[body, witnesses, auxiliary data or null]`, which leaves out the validity flag from Alonzo on (`eras/alonzo/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Alonzo/Tx.hs:377-385` and `:482-496`). A mismatch stops the read with the transaction named.

Every command refuses, before it runs anything, an option it does not define (another command's included, with a suggestion when an option of the command is within two edits, and a hint to write the option and its value as two words when the word holds `=`), a value option with no value (nothing after it, or a word that begins with `--`, which is never a value) and a stray word, which is what a flag given a value leaves. It also refuses an option given twice, flags included, except `--set`, `--wallet`, `--window` and `--history`, which repeat. Each command has its own options: `--from-study` takes a value (`random` or `all`) for `history` and `study`, and is a flag for `census` and `near-cap`; `--validity`, `--oracle`, `--explain`, `--all-wallets`, `--keep-for-debug` and `--cleanup` are flags. The reader also checks the values a typo would change: `--from-study` (`random` or `all`), `--methods` (`study` or `test`), `--bytes` (`tx_cbor` or `immutable`), `--plan` (`recent`, `peak` or `test` for `replay`; `synthetic`, `preprod` or `corpus` for `study`) and `--window`, and requires a positive whole number for `--workers`, `--oracle-workers`, `--reps`, `--reps-h1`, `--reps-variants`, `--test-reps`, `--limit-ms`, `--answer-limit-ms`, `--days` and `--batch`. `--oracle-limit-ms`, which an earlier version took, is refused with its reason. The options are listed once, in `src/options.mjs`, which `study` and `live-fire` read for theirs; their own checks of what a plan or a run uses stay.

Common options: the database options are `--expect-network <name>`, `--snapshot`, `--extraction-run`, `--bytes`, `--immutable-dir`, `--immutable-host`, `--set`, `--treatment` and `--treatment-pins`, and `history`, `census`, `near-cap` and `collateral` take all of them (`--treatment` and `--treatment-pins` are used by `history` and `census` only). `--expect-network <name>` refuses any other database; `--snapshot tip|<block hash>|<block id>` (default `tip`); `--extraction-run <params.json>` takes the snapshot and the windows from an extraction run of `tools/research-corpus` and checks them against the database (the `live-fire` command's `--extraction-run` names a run ID instead; on mainnet both come from the gate: `--snapshot` may name only the gate's block, and `--extraction-run` only the gate's run); `--bytes`, `--immutable-dir`, `--immutable-host`; `--treatment`, `--treatment-pins`; `--set <name>=<value>` changes one setting; `--explain`, for `census` and `collateral` only, keeps the plain `EXPLAIN` plan of their queries; `--analysis-freeze-tx <hash>`, the mainnet transaction whose signed record freezes the analysis scripts; without it a confirmation-split wallet is refused, and with it the run records the hash.

`--from-study` reads the wallets from the extraction's `study` tables, when the role can read them: for `history`, its random sample (`--from-study random`) or every sampled wallet (`--from-study all`); for `census`, its eligible recent-frame wallets; for `near-cap`, the union of both random samples. A wallet the extraction's reconstruction check excluded is refused. `--all-wallets` takes every key stake credential with an output, for a test network.

### The thread cap

A run keeps at most 12 threads busy (`MAX_BUSY_THREADS` in `src/replay/pool.mjs`), its replay or battery workers and its oracle workers together, since the replay limit and the method answer limit are wall-clock limits and an oversubscribed machine moves outcomes near them. `replay` refuses more than 12 `--workers`, and `battery` more than 12 `--workers` and `--oracle-workers` together (the oracle's workers count only with `--oracle`); `study` and `live-fire` refuse such counts before their first stage. A worker count must be a positive whole number. `run.json` records the counts and the cap as `threads`: `{ workers, oracleWorkers, busy, cap }`. The oracle's checks are not study runs, and hold `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_WORKERS` to the same cap.

## Outputs

Wallet-level data never enters the repository. Everything is written under `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-$HOME/.local/share}/unfrackit-research/sim/<network>/<run-id>/` (`RESEARCH_SIM_HOME` overrides the root), directories 0700 and files 0600:

```
history/<wallet>.<window>.jsonl   one wallet and window
history-run.json                  the run's snapshot, parameters, settings, byte source and per-file counts
census.jsonl, census-run.json     the census rows and the run's summary
near-cap.json                     near-cap counts per epoch boundary
collateral.json                   the collateral measurement
battery-pools.json                the T1 amounts and T5 shares
```

Files name wallets by db-sync's stake-credential id (`stake_address.id`); no stake address appears in them.

The `replay` and `battery` commands write, under the same root:

```
run.json         the command, its options and plan, the settings (the oracle's too, with --oracle), the busy threads and their cap, the treatment's digests, the Node version and the code manifest
records.jsonl    the records, one JSON object per line
timings.jsonl    wall-clock times, kept apart so that a run repeated with the same seeds writes byte-identical records
log.jsonl        start, each time-out or error, end
```

Every record names its wallet (the stake-credential id, or a shape's name), window, arm, variant, method, change policy, collateral rule and repetition, and an intent by its position in the wallet's stream. A record that would carry an address, a stake address, a transaction hash or a credential is refused, and free-text fields (a failure's detail, a refusal's reason) have them replaced by placeholders. The kinds:

- `spend`: one replayed intent: its outcome (built, failed with its class and a detail that names any part written after the library priced the transaction and any of the intent's redeemers the transaction lacks, infeasible by balance, infeasible for collateral with its reason, not replayable (an intent the method has no way to express: the H-12 rule's request carries only requested outputs, the test method or `cjs-rrri` meets a body field the kit cannot replay, such as protocol parameter updates, a CSL method meets a part CSL cannot express, or `evo-lf` meets a part its library's types cannot hold), or forfeit), the fee and the wallet-controlled fee, size, inputs, signing keys, assets dragged, change outputs with their bytes and largest Value, the drag bytes of H-2, the bytes and fee per policy recreated in change for H-3, the collateral used (`chosen`: `set-aside`, or `library` for the Evolution SDK's methods), the collateral available under every rule at a script interaction, and, for a built spend and for a forfeit, the state after it: the UTxO count (`utxosAfter`), the largest Value (`largestValueBytesAfter`), the locked ADA (`lockedAdaAfter`), the locked ADA per token (`lockedAdaPerTokenAfter`, `null` when no token is held) and the number of distinct tokens held (`tokensAfter`);
- `plan`: a tidy plan an arm applied, at the start or at a 30-day mark, with its fee, transaction count and UTxOs after;
- `burst`: a parallel burst's separate pass, and whether it was feasible;
- `trigger`: an H-6 trigger, with its rescue outcome under each confirmatory collateral rule;
- `wallet-arm`: per wallet and arm over the window: counts by outcome and class, failures per 100 attempted spends, spend, wallet-controlled, plan and forfeit fees, burst feasibility, the start state (`start`, at time 0: the state the trajectory begins from, after the start plan for an arm that plans one) and the end state (`end`), each with its UTxO count, largest Value, locked ADA, locked ADA per token and token count, the largest Value's trajectory (one point for the start and one for each built spend, forfeit and spend infeasible for collateral, in order; a spend infeasible for collateral carries no state after it, and its point repeats the previous one), the end state's conformity to the arm's layout (`conformity`, below), and H-3's time-averaged locked ADA per policy where the arm records it, and, under `--validity`, the `validity` counts (below);
- `snapshot-plan`: one per wallet that runs the standard battery, under the method name `snapshot-plan`: the transaction count and fee of the planner's default plan of the snapshot state and, for the wallet's token UTxOs that are not held (the plan never spends a held one), their count, coin and minimum ADA before the plan and after it. The fall in the minimum, less the plan's fee, is the ADA the plan recovers; the coin above the minimum on those UTxOs is excess ADA, a separate quantity. It names no address, transaction or asset;
- `battery` and `h12`: one battery run by one method, its outcome, fees, the "balance suffices" flag, the plan after the spend, and for the H-12 rule its completion, chain length and whole-chain fees; the oracle's reference to a standard-battery spend, when an oracle is given, and, under `--validity`, the `validity` counts of its spend's transactions;
- `timeout` and `refused`: a replay that ran past its limit, and one the rules do not settle (a wallet for which the change-address rule names no address where the replay needs one, a start plan the planner refuses).

The `fidelity` command writes `run.json` and `fidelity.json`, its counts and distributions per window, under the private root.

The `live-fire` command writes its stages under `<network>/<run-id>/<stage>/`, as a study does, and deletes `<network>/<run-id>/` when the run ends, unless a failed run is kept for debugging. Its entries go to the extraction tool's check log ("Live-fire checks" below).

The `study` command writes under `<network>/<run-id>/`, where the network is `--expect-network`, or `synthetic`:

```
study.json   the plan, its stages, the options with paths made absolute, the SHA-256 of the treatment pins,
             wallets and extraction-run files, the code manifest (as hash prints it) and its SHA-256, the Node version
log.jsonl    appended: each invocation's start or resume, each stage's start, then its end with the stage's
             printed totals, or its error; the stages skipped; the end
lock         the process ID of the invocation running it
<stage>/     each stage's own run directory, written by its command under the run ID <run-id>/<stage>
```

#### Validity checks

`--validity` reads every transaction a method or plan returns and the kit passes with cardano-serialization-lib, and runs the four negative controls on it. CSL's re-serialization must have the transaction's inputs, fee and witness count and its outputs by decoded content. CSL writes a policy's asset names shortest first and re-encodes some fixed parts (certificates, witness lists) as tagged sets, so an output's bytes or the transaction's hash can differ. Both are counted (`cslOutputBytes`, `cslReencoded`) and neither is a failure. The controls (`src/kit/controls.mjs`, which the tests import through `test/lib/controls.mjs`) are the fee one lovelace under the ledger minimum with the difference moved into the last output, the size one byte over the limit, a Value one byte over the value-size limit, and one vkey witness short; each must be caught by its own check alone. A transaction can have no outputs: a library burns a leftover below the change minimum into the fee, and the intent requests nothing. It has no output to take the fee difference and none whose Value could be too large, so it has no fee or value control and is counted in `notApplicable`.

A replay's `wallet-arm` record and a battery run's record gain `validity: { txs, cslRead, cslOutputBytes, cslReencoded, controls: { fee, size, value, witnesses }, notApplicable: { fee, value }, failed }`, with `controls` counting the transactions each control was caught on and `cslRead` those CSL read and re-serialized to the same inputs, fee, witness count and outputs. A replay counts the transactions of its spends, of the plans its arm applies and of the burst pass, so a plan's transactions are counted under the job's selector although the planner built them; a battery run counts its spend's transactions. A failure adds `failures` (up to 20 entries: where it came from, the transaction's position in the answer, the check and a scrubbed detail, never a hash). It writes one `validity failure` note to `log.jsonl` and makes the command exit with status 1 after it has printed its totals, which carry the sums. The option adds no other field and changes none (`test/treatment/validity.test.mjs`).

### History files

One JSON Lines file per wallet and window. Amounts are decimal strings; hashes, policies, names and CBOR lowercase hex; references `<transaction id>#<index>`.

1. `header`: the wallet, frame and split; `sample`, the wallet's membership as `{ recentRandom, peakRandom, recentStratum }`: each frame's random sample and the recent frame's purposive strata (null when the study tables were not read or the wallet is in no sample); the window's first and last blocks, its transaction-id range [`lo`, `hi`] and the transaction id of its start state; the snapshot; the snapshot epoch's protocol parameters; the franken-flagged payment credentials; the arrival table; `latestKeyBase`, the latest output the wallet received at or before the start state's transaction, spent or not, at a key payment credential and a base address whose credential is not franken-flagged, as `{ address, txId, index }` (null when there is none); the settings; the byte source and db-sync version.
2. `state`: the UTxO set as of the last block before the window's first block, as UTxO records.
3. Events in chain order:
   - `deposit`: the wallet outputs created by a transaction that is not the wallet's spend, as UTxO records;
   - `held-consumed`: the held wallet UTxOs consumed by a transaction that is not the wallet's spend;
   - `intent`: a spend and its intent;
   - `self-reorganization`: a spend with no requested output, no certificate, vote or proposal, no mint or burn and no script; counted, its reshuffle of the wallet's inputs not replayed. It carries its implicit values, the held UTxOs it consumed, `withdrawal` (its reward withdrawals, which the replay deposits), the net value it brought in from outside the wallet other than those, and the observed wallet parts;
   - `mark`: every 30 days of block time from the window's first block, placed before the first event at or after its time.
4. `end`: the UTxO set's count and digest at the window's last block, and the event counts.

A UTxO record is `{ ref, cbor, address, cred, coin, assets, held, origin, txId }`: `cbor` is the CIP-30 `TransactionUnspentOutput` (CIP-0030, "TransactionUnspentOutput": `[input, output]`), `cred` the payment credential, `assets` `[{ policy, name, qty }]` sorted by policy then name bytes, `held` the reason the UTxO is held (`script-payment`, `datum`, `reference-script` or `franken`) or null, `origin` `initial` or `deposit`, and `txId` db-sync's id of the creating transaction, which follows chain order and ranks outputs for the change address. The output inside `cbor` is re-encoded from db-sync's columns on every network, so that preprod runs the code mainnet runs. The address bytes come from its text and the value takes the ledger's serialization. A datum is its hash or the inline datum's bytes. A reference script comes from db-sync's script bytes, or, for a native script, whose bytes db-sync does not keep, from its creating transaction's bytes. db-sync does not record whether the original output used the array or the map form, so the re-encoding uses the map form only when an inline datum or a reference script needs it; its content always equals the original's.

An intent is `{ id, source, type, time, requested, implicit, fixed, collateral, invalid, burst, observed, classes, derived }`:

- `time`: slot, block time (Unix seconds), index in the block, epoch, block number.
- `requested`: `[{ cbor, address, coin, assets, datum, scriptRef, raised, index, wallet, scriptPayment }]`, with `from` and `inflow` on a requested wallet output built from an inflow. `raised` is null or `{ from, to, rule, original }`.
- `implicit`: `{ withdrawals, refunds, deposits, donation, mint, burn }`.
- `fixed`: null, or `{ inputs, referenceInputs, body, witnesses, redeemers, auxiliaryData, scriptFee, fixedBytes, refScriptBytes }`. `inputs` and `referenceInputs` are `[{ ref, cbor }]`, a held wallet input carrying `held` and, at a key payment credential, `walletKey`; `body` maps Conway CDDL body field numbers to CBOR; `witnesses` maps witness-set keys to CBOR; a redeemer is `{ tag, target, data, exUnits }`.
- `collateral`: `{ required, observed }`, `observed` null or `{ inputs, return, total }`.
- `invalid`: null, or for a phase-2-invalid transaction `{ forfeit, heldConsumed }`.
- `burst`: null or `{ id, parallel, index }`.
- `observed`: `{ tx, size, fee, epoch, protocolMajor, minFeeA, minFeeB, walletInputs, walletOutputs, walletWitnesses }`.
- `classes`: each asset of the transaction's wallet inputs, outputs and requested outputs, `FT` or `NFT`.
- `derived`: null, or the pool deposits taken from db-sync's net deposit.

## The study run

`study` runs the commands above as one chain under one run ID, each stage in its own run directory, so that a run of many hours can stop and resume. Three plans:

| Plan | Stages, in order | Wallets, snapshot and windows |
|---|---|---|
| `synthetic` | `replay-recent`, `replay-peak`, `battery` | The shapes `--shapes` names, with their synthetic streams and fixed battery values. No database. |
| `preprod` | `history-recent`, `history-peak`, `history-snapshot`, `census`, `near-cap`, `collateral`, `fidelity`, `battery-pools`, `replay-recent`, `replay-peak`, `battery` | A test network (`--expect-network`, never mainnet). The wallets from `--wallets`, `--wallet` or `--from-study random\|all`. The snapshot from `--snapshot <block id or hash>` or `--extraction-run`, never the tip, so that every stage reads one block. The windows from `--extraction-run`, or computed from the snapshot. `--days` for the collateral measurement. |
| `corpus` | as `preprod` | Every sampled wallet (`--from-study all`), with `--analysis-freeze-tx` required, since the confirmation split is read. The snapshot and windows are the extraction run's: on mainnet the gate's, on a test network `--extraction-run`'s. |

Each stage runs one command:

- `history-recent`, `history-peak` and `history-snapshot`: `history` for that window;
- `census` and `near-cap` over the wallets given, or with `--from-study` for the corpus (the census over the recent frame's eligible wallets, near-cap over both random samples); `collateral`;
- `fidelity`: over the recent and peak history files of the exploration split, and those that carry no split, as on a test network;
- `battery-pools`: over `history-recent`'s files;
- `replay-recent` and `replay-peak`: `replay --plan recent` and `--plan peak` over that window's history files, or over the shapes' streams;
- `battery`: over `history-snapshot`'s files with the pools `battery-pools` wrote, or over the shapes.

The database stages come first, so a run needs the database only at its start. `--reps-h1`, `--reps-variants` and `--limit-ms` pass to the replays; `--reps`, `--answer-limit-ms`, `--oracle`, `--oracle-workers` and `--oracle-settings` to the battery; `--workers` and `--validity` to both; `--analysis-freeze-tx` to `history`, the replays and the battery; `--treatment` and `--treatment-pins` (default `data/treatment-pins.txt`) to every stage that loads the treatment; the connection's options (`--expect-network`, `--snapshot`, `--extraction-run`, `--bytes`, `--immutable-dir`, `--immutable-host`, `--set`) to every database stage. An option the plan does not use is refused, and so are worker counts that would keep more than 12 threads busy in the battery stage, before any stage starts.

Running the same plan and run ID again resumes the run. Its options (paths made absolute), the SHA-256 of its input files and the code manifest must equal those of its first start, or it is refused, naming what differs; that includes `--workers`. A stage whose end entry is in the log is skipped. Every other stage reruns from its start, its directory removed first, so its files are written as by an uninterrupted run, and a stage stopped halfway loses its partial work. The records then equal an uninterrupted run's, except where a stage's records vary between any two runs: the unseeded selectors (`csl-ri`, `csl-ri-pure`) and their runs kept beside each oracle reference, the answers at the answer limit and the references of their spend and policy, and any reference the oracle's guard stopped. A second invocation on a run ID that is running is refused; the lock of a process that has gone is taken over. A stage that throws stops the run, with its error in the log. A stage that finishes with a failed validity check is logged as failed, the run goes on, and the command exits with status 1 at the end.

The study's mainnet run, after the final freeze:

```
node src/cli.mjs study --plan corpus --run-id <id> --expect-network mainnet \
  --treatment <dir> --treatment-pins data/treatment-pins.txt \
  --analysis-freeze-tx <hash> \
  --immutable-dir <the node's ImmutableDB directory> --immutable-host <host> \
  --oracle --workers 8 --oracle-workers 4
```

It runs at the study's repetitions, limits and oracle budget (the commands' defaults) with 12 busy threads, the most the commands allow: 8 replay or battery workers and 4 oracle workers. Its connection (`PGHOST` and the rest) is mainnet's db-sync, through a role that can read the `study` schema.

## Running jobs unattended

`scripts/run-queue.mjs` runs a list of commands one after another without anyone watching, and records what happened so that a stopped list resumes where it left off. It runs commands and nothing else: the simulator's own commands enforce their thread caps and limits, and the runner adds none of its own beyond a wall-clock limit per job. It uses only Node's built-ins.

The method answer limit and the replay limit are wall-clock limits, so a long run's outcomes depend on what else the machine is doing. Keep a long run the only load on the machine. A job's `idle` setting makes the runner wait until the one-minute load average has stayed low before it starts the job, and fail the job if that does not happen in time.

A queue is a JSON file. This one runs a replay and then a battery on the synthetic shapes, with the output location in an env file:

```
{
  "name": "synthetic-check",
  "out": "queue-out",
  "envFile": "run.env",
  "jobs": [
    {
      "id": "replay",
      "cwd": "/path/to/research-sim",
      "argv": ["node", "src/cli.mjs", "replay", "--treatment", "/path/to/treatment", "--shapes", "all", "--plan", "test", "--run-id", "synthetic-replay", "--workers", "2"],
      "timeoutMinutes": 120,
      "idle": { "maxLoad1": 1.0, "forMinutes": 5, "maxWaitMinutes": 60 },
      "pauseAfter": true
    },
    {
      "id": "battery",
      "cwd": "/path/to/research-sim",
      "argv": ["node", "src/cli.mjs", "battery", "--treatment", "/path/to/treatment", "--shapes", "all", "--run-id", "synthetic-battery", "--workers", "2"],
      "timeoutMinutes": 120
    }
  ]
}
```

`run.env` holds lines such as `export RESEARCH_SIM_HOME=/path/to/output`. A relative `out`, `cwd` or `envFile` is resolved against the queue file's directory.

The queue file is checked whole before anything runs. An unknown key, a missing or mistyped field, a duplicate id, a `cwd` that does not exist and a timeout that is not positive are refused with the path of the field, and nothing runs. The top level has `name`, `out` (the directory for the state file and logs), `jobs` and optionally `env` and `envFile`, which apply to every job. A job has:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | A safe file name, unique in the queue. The job's logs go under `out/<id>/`. |
| `cwd` | The directory the job runs in. |
| `argv` | The command and its arguments, as an array. No shell is involved. |
| `timeoutMinutes` | Required. The job's wall-clock limit. |
| `env`, `envFile` | Environment for this job. |
| `idle` | `maxLoad1`, `forMinutes` and `maxWaitMinutes`, all required: wait until the one-minute load average has stayed at or under `maxLoad1` for `forMinutes`, and fail the job if that has not happened within `maxWaitMinutes`. |
| `before`, `after` | Commands (argv arrays) run in the job's directory and environment, each under the job's limit. `before` must exit 0, or the job fails without running. `after` runs once the job has started or failed, whatever the outcome, and its exit status is recorded without changing the job's status. A runner stopped by a signal does not run it. |
| `pauseAfter` | Stop the queue once this job is done. |

An env file holds lines `KEY=VALUE` or `export KEY=VALUE`, comments starting with `#`, and blank lines. A value is everything after the first `=`, taken literally: no quotes are interpreted and nothing is expanded. Any other line is refused. A job's variables override the queue's, and both override the environment the runner inherited; within one level, `env` overrides `envFile`.

```
node scripts/run-queue.mjs run <queue.json> [--continue-on-failure] [--rerun-interrupted] [--detach]
node scripts/run-queue.mjs status <queue.json>
```

`run` takes a lock file in `out`, holding the runner's process ID, so that one runner serves a queue: it is refused while that process lives and taken over, with a message, when the process is gone. Jobs run in order, one at a time, each in its own process group with standard output and standard error in `out/<id>/attempt-<n>/stdout.log` and `stderr.log` (`before.log` and `after.log` beside them). When a job passes its limit, the whole group receives SIGTERM and, if anything is left 30 seconds later, SIGKILL, and the job is `timeout`. A non-zero exit or a signal makes it `failed`. The queue stops at the first job that is not done, unless `--continue-on-failure` is given.

To resume, run the same command again. A job recorded `done` is skipped; a `failed` or `timeout` job runs again as a new attempt. `--detach` starts the runner in a new session with its output in `out/runner.log`, prints its process ID and returns. `status` prints one line per job (id, status, attempts, last duration and exit) and whether a runner holds the lock.

SIGINT or SIGTERM to the runner is passed to the running job's group, the job is recorded `interrupted` and the lock is released. A job recorded as running whose process is gone (the runner was killed, or the machine restarted) is marked `interrupted` the next time the runner starts, and the runner refuses to go on until it is started with `--rerun-interrupted`, which runs the job again as a new attempt. The choice is left to the operator because a rerun can meet a half-written run directory; look at it first. If an interrupted job's process is still alive, the runner refuses to start.

Exit codes of `run`:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Every job is done. |
| 1 | A job failed or timed out. |
| 2 | The queue is invalid, or another runner holds the lock. |
| 3 | The queue paused after a `pauseAfter` job, with jobs left. |
| 4 | An interrupted job awaits `--rerun-interrupted`, or a signal interrupted a job. |

`out/state.json` is rewritten atomically (a temporary file renamed over it) at every transition, so it can be read while the queue runs. For each job it holds the status (`pending`, `waiting-idle`, `running`, `done`, `failed`, `timeout` or `interrupted`) and, for each attempt, the start and end times, the duration, the exit code or signal, the process ID, the load average at the start, the exit status of `before` and `after`, the command and the names (never the values) of the variables set from the queue. For each runner start it records the CPU model, logical core count, total memory, operating system and Node version, and, for each job directory inside a git work tree, the commit and whether tracked files had uncommitted changes or untracked files were present, so that a reader comparing timings knows what produced them.

## Live-fire checks

Before the final freeze, the study's code may run on mainnet wallets to confirm that it works, under a commitment that nothing such a run shows changes the pre-registration. The extraction tool's live-fire run with `--sim-wallets` picks the wallets from its draw order under a stated test value of h, at most ten, and keeps the pick in `sim/wallets.json` in its run directory until its own `cleanup`. On mainnet it picks once. The simulator's live-fire runs use that pick:

```
live-fire --run-id sim-<id> --extraction-run <id> --purpose <text> --expect-network <name>
          --treatment <dir> [--treatment-pins <file>] [--workers <n>] [--oracle-workers <n>]
          [--bytes tx_cbor|immutable] [--immutable-dir <dir>] [--immutable-host <host>] [--keep-for-debug]
live-fire --cleanup --run-id sim-<id> --network <name>
```

`--extraction-run` here names the extraction live-fire run by its run ID, not a `params.json` file. The run ID begins with `sim-`, so that neither tool's entries settle the other's runs. `--workers` (default 1) and `--oracle-workers` (default 1) together may keep at most 12 threads busy. Any option the command does not define is refused, and so is an option given twice. The pick fixes the snapshot block and the test value of h, so no option names them.

A run goes in this order:

1. The options, then the pick, checked as the gate's live-fire path checks it, against the check log of `--expect-network`. A refusal here has queried nothing and adds nothing to the log.
2. The gate, logged as a query: a `query-start` entry named `sim live-fire gate`, then a connection that reads db-sync's `meta`, refuses a database other than `--expect-network` and on mainnet takes the gate's live-fire path, then a `query` entry with its outcome. A refused gate is a failed query, never a live-fire run.
3. The `sim-live-fire-start` entry, written only after the gate has passed and before any wallet is read: the run ID, the gate's entry, the command line, the purpose, the test value of h, the extraction run, the code (the SHA-256 of the code manifest and the Node version), and the plan (the stages, the repetitions, the limits with the oracle's guard, the oracle's work budget, the workers).
4. The stages, in the study's layout: each in its own run directory, `<run-id>/<stage>`, run by the command the study runs for it. `history-recent`, `history-peak` and `history-snapshot` read every picked wallet at the pick's snapshot block, with the windows computed from that block. `battery-pools` reads the recent files. `replay-recent` and `replay-peak` run their window's plan, and `battery` runs the standard battery with the study's selectors and the oracle. Validity checks are on throughout. Each arm, variant and random selector runs one repetition, and the limits are the study's: 300 seconds per replay, 60 seconds per method answer, and the oracle's work budget per reference. A stage that throws stops the run. A stage that finishes with a failed validity check is a failed step, and the run goes on.
5. The `sim-live-fire` end entry, naming the start entry by `start_seq`.

The end entry holds the outcome (`succeeded` or `failed`), the error, the run time, and each step's name, outcome, class of error and time. A class of error is `refusal`, `history-check` (the history reader's checks of a wallet's transactions, its intents and its reconstruction), `database`, `connection`, `check` (a failed validity check) or the error's kind. It also holds three checks over the whole run, never per wallet. Each is true or false, or null when the stages that settle it did not all finish:

- `every_intent_parsed`: every history stage finished, so every spend in both windows became its event and every history file was completed. It is false when a history stage stopped on one of the reader's checks.
- `every_built_transaction_passed_ledger_checks`: no replay or battery stage counted a validity failure. Every transaction a method or plan built and the kit passed was then read by CSL to the same inputs, fee, witness count and outputs, and each of its four negative controls was caught by its own check.
- `replay_reached_limit`: a replay ran past the 300-second limit.

The entry records no fee, size, count of failures or other outcome, and no stake address, address, policy or transaction hash. The error text passes through the extraction tool's redaction, applied in full to every error, this code's own refusals included: bech32 strings and long hex runs become `<bech32>` and `<hex>`, and every number becomes `#`. The progress lines name steps and times only, and the command prints the outcome, the steps, the checks and the log entries' numbers, nothing counted.

The last field, `wallet_level_output`, says what became of the run's directory: its history files, records and logs. The command deletes it when the run ends, whether the run succeeded or failed (`deleted <time>`, or `deletion failed: <error>`, or `none written`). With `--keep-for-debug`, a run in which a step failed keeps it instead (`kept for debugging`); a run that succeeds keeps nothing. Only the failed step's output is then read, to debug it, and once the fix is committed, `live-fire --cleanup --run-id <id> --network <name>` deletes the directory and adds a `cleanup` entry under the run's ID, with the fields the extraction tool's `cleanup` writes. A run killed midway leaves its start entry unclosed and its directory behind; the extraction tool's `check-log verify` reports it until the same run ID ends with its output deleted or `--cleanup` runs, which closes the start. The extraction run's `sim/wallets.json` is the extraction tool's, and only its `cleanup` deletes it.

The entries go to `check-log/<network>.jsonl` under the extraction tool's root, `RESEARCH_CORPUS_HOME` or `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-$HOME/.local/share}/unfrackit-research`, in that tool's format: one JSON object per line, appended, each with its sequence number and the SHA-256 of the file before it (`src/study/checklog.mjs`). The extraction tool reads them as live-fire runs: its final-freeze check takes the first live-fire start of either tool as the first live-fire run, which must follow the hypotheses record's block. Two runs appending to one log at once could write the same sequence number, so the live-fire runs and the extraction tool's commands run one at a time.

The mainnet run is the study author's, made by hand only after the hypotheses record is on chain and the extraction's mainnet live-fire run has picked the wallets:

```
node src/cli.mjs live-fire --run-id sim-<id> --extraction-run <the extraction run that picked> \
  --purpose <text> --expect-network mainnet \
  --treatment <dir> --treatment-pins data/treatment-pins.txt \
  --immutable-dir <the node's ImmutableDB directory> --immutable-host <host> \
  --workers 8 --oracle-workers 4
```

Its connection is mainnet's db-sync, through a role that can read `study.run` and `study.param`, and the run reads transaction bytes from the node's ImmutableDB, as the study does. On preprod the same command runs with `--expect-network preprod`, against preprod's check log and an extraction live-fire run on preprod.

## The rules this code implements

A wallet is a stake credential. Its outputs are those db-sync gives its `stake_address_id`: outputs at base addresses with its stake credential, whatever their payment credential, and outputs at pointer addresses created before Conway (CIP-0019, "Delegation part" and "Pointers"). Byron outputs never belong to a wallet. A wallet's UTxO set at transaction id T is every output created at or before T and not consumed at or before T, read from the wallet's own rows. Before a wallet is read, that set at the snapshot is compared with db-sync's own UTxO-at-block pattern restricted to the wallet, and a wallet whose two sets differ is refused.

Held UTxOs are kept and flagged, never offered to a selector: a UTxO at a script payment credential, one carrying a datum or a reference script, and one at a franken-flagged payment credential. Anyone can create an output that pairs their own payment key with another wallet's stake credential (CIP-0019, "Delegation part"), so such a credential may not be the wallet's; the study calls these franken addresses. In a wallet with two or more key payment credentials, a credential is flagged if it never appears as an input in a transaction that also spends another of the wallet's key payment credentials. The rule reads the wallet's whole history up to the snapshot, and flags nothing in a wallet where no pair of credentials co-spends.

A spend event is a transaction that consumes a wallet output at a key payment credential. Its intent:

- **Requested outputs.** Every output to another stake credential, and every wallet output at a script payment credential or carrying a datum or a reference script, as observed. From the wallet's other outputs, the net inflow of each native asset that did not come from a wallet input (it came from a non-wallet input or a mint) becomes requested wallet outputs at their minimum, each at the address of the observed output that received it. Every other wallet output is change. A requested output whose coin is below the minimum at the snapshot (coins per UTxO byte at the snapshot times 160 plus the output's size as written, the ledger's rule and the kit's) is raised to it, the raise recorded with the minimum-UTxO rule of its transaction's era: Mary (protocol major 4 and below), Alonzo (5 and 6), or Babbage and Conway (7 on).
- **Implicit values.** Withdrawals, refunds, deposits, the treasury donation, mints and burns, read from the transaction's fields and checked against db-sync's `withdrawal`, `tx.deposit`, `ma_tx_mint` and `tx.treasury_donation`. Deposits follow each certificate's rule in the Conway CDDL (`conway.cddl:30-48`, `:429-540`): a Shelley-era registration or deregistration at the epoch's key deposit, Conway's registration and deregistration certificates and DRep certificates with their stated coin, and proposals with theirs. A pool registration pays the pool deposit only when the pool is new, which the bytes do not say; its deposit is the remainder of db-sync's net deposit, which must be a whole number of pool deposits.
- **Fixed parts**, each as its original bytes:
  - non-wallet inputs and held wallet inputs, with their resolved outputs, and reference inputs;
  - every body field except the inputs, outputs and fee, the two hashes a builder recomputes (7 and 11), and collateral (13, 16 and 17);
  - the witness-set entries other than the wallet's own payment-key witnesses;
  - the redeemers, each with the item it points at, resolved in the ledger's order: inputs by transaction id then index, policies by bytes, reward accounts by network then credential (a script credential before a key) then hash, voters by kind (committee, DRep, pool) then credential, certificates and proposals as listed;
  - the auxiliary data.

  The script fee is the execution term `ceiling(priceMem * mem + priceStep * steps)` (`libs/cardano-ledger-core/src/Cardano/Ledger/Plutus/ExUnits.hs:198-203`) plus the Conway reference-script term, both under the snapshot's parameters. The reference-script term's price per byte grows by 1.2 for every further 25,600 bytes (`eras/conway/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Conway/Tx.hs:105-145`), over the reference scripts of the inputs and reference inputs (`eras/conway/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Conway/UTxO.hs:149-171`). The bytes of the fixed parts are the observed size less the size of the same transaction with them removed.
- **Collateral** is required when the transaction carries a redeemer or is phase-2-invalid; the observed collateral is kept for reporting. A phase-2-invalid transaction is an intent whose only effect is the loss of its total collateral.
- **Spend type**, in this order: T6 script execution (a Plutus redeemer, or phase-2-invalid; native scripts never count), T8 mint or burn, T7 certificate or governance, T5 fungible tokens sent, T4 NFTs of several policies, T3 several NFTs of one policy, T2 one NFT, T1 pure-ADA payment. An asset's class: a valid CIP-67 label decides (333 and 444 fungible; 222, 100 and 500 non-fungible unless more than one unit is held), otherwise more than one unit makes it fungible (CIP-0067, "Specification"; CIP-0068, "Labels", "333 FT Standard", "444 RFT Standard"). The units are the largest of the wallet's holding at the window's start, at its end, and the units the transaction moved.

Every intent is checked before it is written. db-sync's consumed inputs (the body inputs, or the collateral inputs of a phase-2-invalid transaction) and its outputs must be the transaction's. Coin and every asset must be conserved with the implicit values. The transaction rebuilt from the intent's parts and the observed wallet inputs, change and witnesses, each part's original bytes, must have the transaction's exact body hash and size. Before a history file is complete, its observed events replayed from the start state must reach the wallet's UTxO set at the window's end. Any failure stops the run with the transaction named.

A burst is a maximal run of the wallet's spends with no more than 60 seconds between consecutive block times. It is chained if any of its transactions spends an output of an earlier transaction of the same burst, transactions in one block ordered by their index; parallel otherwise.

For every asset held at a window's start, the arrival table names the transaction in which it first entered one of the wallet's outputs over the whole history, or the creating transaction of the UTxO holding it, flagged, when none is found.

The census measures value bytes as the ledger's serialization of the Value (`eras/alonzo/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Alonzo/Rules/Utxo.hs:425-447`) and a UTxO's minimum with CSL on the output re-encoded from db-sync's columns. The value-size limit in force is `max_val_size` from Alonzo on, and the Mary era's 4,000-byte constant before it (`eras/allegra/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Allegra/Rules/Utxo.hs:249-264`).

## The simulator core

### The ledger kit

Every size is the ledger's size of the transaction's bytes, never an estimate, and every fee is `minFeeA * size + minFeeB`, plus for a replayed script spend the intent's script fee (`eras/alonzo/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Alonzo/Tx.hs:498-508`; `eras/conway/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Conway/Tx.hs:105-145`). The wallet-controlled fee, which every fee comparison uses, is that fee less the script fee and less `minFeeA` times the bytes of the intent's fixed parts.

Every built transaction is checked from its bytes, without CSL, so that the checks are a second implementation beside the treatment's and the libraries' own: the size limit (`eras/shelley/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Shelley/Rules/Utxo.hs:570-585`); every output's Value, the collateral return's included, at most `maxValSize` (`eras/alonzo/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Alonzo/Rules/Utxo.hs:425-447`); every output's coin at least `coinsPerUtxoByte * (160 + its bytes)` (`eras/babbage/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Babbage/TxOut.hs:678-702`); every input resolving; coin and every asset conserved with the withdrawals, refunds, mints, deposits, burns and treasury donation (`Shelley/Rules/Utxo.hs:513-529`; `eras/conway/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Conway/UTxO.hs:107-145`); the fee at least the minimum (`eras/babbage/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Babbage/Rules/Utxo.hs:183-212`); one vkey witness per distinct key payment credential among the inputs and collateral inputs, plus the intent's other witnesses (`eras/alonzo/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Alonzo/TxBody.hs:307-313`; `eras/shelley/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Shelley/UTxO.hs:205-227`); the auxiliary-data hash present exactly with auxiliary data; and, with redeemers, collateral: one to `maxCollateralInputs` inputs at key addresses, the balance less the return ADA only, at least `collateralPercent` of the fee, and equal to the total-collateral field (`Babbage/Rules/Utxo.hs:231-308`; `eras/babbage/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Babbage/Collateral.hs:30-39`). A failed check is a build failure of one class: transaction size, value size, change minimum, maximum input count, balance, fee under the minimum, collateral, time limit or other. A fee under the minimum (`fee-minimum`) is reported apart from balance shortfalls: the transaction balances, and the network rejects it for its fee alone.

A selection-only method's transaction is assembled with CSL: its inputs sorted by transaction id then index, its outputs, and synthetic vkey witnesses, one per distinct payment key, of the size real ones have, at the minimum fee found as a fixed point (the fee's and the change coin's encodings are part of the size). A replayed intent's transaction is assembled from its parts: the requested outputs and every fixed part as their original bytes, the arm's inputs and change, spend redeemers re-indexed to their inputs' places, the auxiliary-data hash recomputed, and collateral under the arm's rule with its return output and total. The script-data hash is a 32-byte stand-in over the redeemers and datums: the ledger's also covers the cost models' language views, which a history file does not carry, and no check reads it. Four negative controls are caught on every form of transaction, each by its own check: the fee one lovelace short, the size one byte over, a Value one byte over, and one witness short.

### States, the treatment and the methods

A state holds the wallet's UTxO records (in `txid#index` order), its stake credential, its franken-flagged credentials and its change address. The change address is the address of the latest output at a key payment credential and a base address, by creating transaction then index and passing over franken-flagged credentials, among every output the wallet has received on the chain up to that point: `latestKeyBase`, then the deposits and the observed wallet outputs of the window's spends and self-reorganizations, the real wallet's own change included. Every arm and method therefore has the same change address at a given spend, and it moves as later outputs arrive; an event's own outputs count from the next event on. A wallet that spends, or holds a spendable UTxO, before it has received such an output, or never receives one, is refused; before that output its state holds nothing a plan could spend, so plans there are recorded empty and not run. Applying a transaction removes its wallet inputs and adds its outputs that carry the wallet's stake credential; payments to other stake credentials leave the wallet.

The treatment is loaded from the directory the operator names, every file's digest checked against `data/treatment-pins.txt` first. Its planner and its H-12 selection rule run on states, given the UTxOs in `txid#index` order with held UTxOs marked held, and every transaction they build must pass both the treatment's own checks and the kit's; a disagreement stops the run. Protocol parameters reach it as its parameters object, and a parameter it would read differently from the run's is refused.

A method is `{ name, version, deterministic, seeded, build(state, intent, params, opts) }`. `deterministic` says that it draws nothing at random, and `seeded` that its answer is fixed by its inputs and the seed it is given: true for every deterministic method and for `cjs-rrri`, which draws from that seed, and false for `csl-ri` and `csl-ri-pure`, whose library draws from a generator no caller can seed, so that their runs cannot be repeated. The contract refuses a deterministic method that is not seeded, and the oracle takes incumbents only from seeded methods. `opts` names a seed, a change policy, the arm's collateral rule, H-12's absorption, for a replayed script spend the collateral the engine has set aside, and in a replay the job's random source (`opts.random`). A method whose library chooses its own collateral declares `ownCollateral` and is given none; only the Evolution SDK's `evo-lf` and the survey's `evo-unfrack` may (`choosesOwnCollateral` in `src/methods/contract.mjs`). Methods are made by name (`src/methods/registry.mjs`), so that a worker thread can make the one its job names: the six baselines `csl-lf`, `csl-ri`, `csl-lf-pure`, `csl-ri-pure`, `cjs-rrri` and `evo-lf`, each made from the loaded treatment at its library's defaults with no options at creation (a build still takes `opts`), the H-12 method and the two test methods. The builder survey's other subjects (`evo-unfrack`, Mesh's) are not registered, so no run plan can name them. The adapters load with the registry, so every worker loads them: a worker's V8 heap after loading the treatment and the registry is about 49 MB, against 12 MB with the treatment alone. The H-12 method serves the batteries only, since its request carries only requested outputs. The two test methods exist only to test the engine and never appear in a study run: `test-largest-first` (largest coin first, with a simulator-owned change policy) and `test-stall`, which blocks its thread to force the time limits. The simulator's change policies, put on a baseline's input selection, are built from the treatment's own functions: `layout` (H-9's layout-preserving change), `single` (one change output, failing at the value-size limit) and `naive` (the naive-tidy control's rule applied to change).

### Arms and run plans

An arm is a starting layout at the window start and how it is kept: `observed`; `tidy-once` (the planner's full plan applied, its fees charged); `maintained-tidy` (as tidy-once, then re-planned at every 30-day mark, any plan applied and charged); and H-1's two controls, `naive-tidy` and `random-tidy`, each applied once. Plan fees are recorded apart from spend fees. A variant is the planner's settings varied as one hypothesis names, every other setting at its default: H-2's groupings by arrival, by policy then arrival and at random, and byte-optimal packing; H-3's bundle sizes 1, 5, 10, 30, 60 and no count cap; H-4's isolation per asset and per policy; H-5's reserve rules R0 to R6; and H-6's never-propose arm, 1 ADA dust threshold and 3-to-6 and 5-to-20 ADA collateral bands.

A run plan lists which arms run in which window under which selectors and change policies. In both windows: H-1's three arms (observed, tidy-once and maintained-tidy) under `csl-lf`, `csl-ri` and `cjs-rrri`; H-1's two controls under `cjs-rrri`, the primary selector; the observed arm under `evo-lf`; and H-5's reserve rules (maintained-tidy) under `cjs-rrri`, `csl-lf` and `csl-ri`. In the recent window also: H-2, H-3 and H-4's variants (tidy-once) and H-6's (maintained-tidy), each under `cjs-rrri` and, as a sensitivity, `csl-lf`; and H-9's tidy-once arm under `cjs-rrri`, with the default and the layout-preserving change, against the maintained-tidy arm under `cjs-rrri` with the default change (the same job as H-1's, whose record lists H-9 as well). In the peak window also: H-11's factorial, the observed arm under each of `csl-lf`, `csl-ri` and `cjs-rrri` crossed with the single, layout-preserving and naive change (`src/arms/plan.mjs`, `windowPlan`). At the snapshot, the battery runs `csl-lf`, `csl-ri`, `cjs-rrri` and `evo-lf`, the sensitivity selectors on change policy (`csl-lf-pure` and `csl-ri-pure`, CSL with `prefer_pure_change` set, each scored against the oracle's `csl-ppc` reference), and the H-12 rule with absorption on and off (`batteryPlan`); no replay runs those two selectors. A random selector runs 30 repetitions in H-1's arms and at the snapshot and 10 in the variants and H-11; a deterministic one runs once. Repetition r runs a seeded selector with seed r and any random layout with seed r, so compared arms share one seed sequence. A replay job, one (wallet, window, arm, variant, selector, change policy, collateral rule, seed), makes one mulberry32 generator from its seed when it starts, and every build of the job draws from it in the order the engine makes them, the burst pass included (`jobOptions` in `src/replay/engine.mjs`), so arms compared with each other start from the same stream whichever worker runs them; a caller cannot give a replay a generator of its own. A battery run, one (spend, selector, repetition), instead seeds a fresh generator with its seed, since every battery spend is built from the same snapshot state, a fresh worker resumes a wallet by spend number after a time-out, and the observed and tidy layouts' H-10 spends then share their draws spend by spend. Each arm runs under the ledger and the practice collateral rule, with the ledger minimum and token-bearing collateral as sensitivity arms, when its window holds a script interaction.

### The replay engine

Each (wallet, arm, variant, selector, change policy, collateral rule, repetition) replay runs in a worker thread, at most 300 seconds of wall-clock time; CSL's calls are synchronous, so a replay past its limit is stopped by terminating its thread, recorded as a time-out, and the pool starts a fresh worker. Workers are also replaced after 40 jobs, since CSL's asm.js heap only grows to the largest wallet a worker has built for; a worker on the 3,005-UTxO shapes holds about 0.8 GiB, so `--workers` should leave that much memory per worker. Deposits enter every arm when they occurred; held UTxOs leave every arm when anything consumes them; self-reorganizations are counted and their reshuffles not replayed, but one that carries a reward withdrawal deposits the withdrawn ADA in every arm, as one UTxO at the change address in effect at it, so that every arm receives what the real wallet received, its fee charged to no arm; at a mark the maintained-tidy arm re-plans. Every arm replays the same intents in the same order, each without two parts that the observed transaction's builder added and the spend does not need: the network id (body field 15), and an auxiliary data holding no metadata and no script, in any of its three encodings, with its hash (body field 7). The ledger requires neither. They are left out before any method, feasibility test or check sees the intent (`withoutLeftovers` in `src/kit/replay.mjs`), and the intent's count of fixed bytes drops by their size. A library that writes either part itself keeps it and pays for it in its fee. The history file keeps both, and the reader's rebuild check rebuilds the observed transaction with them. In each arm:

- A phase-2-invalid intent forfeits the observed total collateral from collateral chosen under the arm's rule, its return output coming back to the wallet. With no eligible UTxO it is infeasible for collateral.
- An intent the arm's balance cannot fund, by the H-12 rule's exact test with the intent's implicit values and fixed inputs counted, is recorded as infeasible by balance and is not a selector failure.
- A script spend sets its collateral aside before any method selects (`setAside` in `src/replay/engine.mjs`), as a wallet with a dedicated collateral UTxO does: the UTxO the arm's rule chooses leaves the pool the method selects from, and every transaction the method assembles carries it as collateral. A script spend with no UTxO to set aside (reason `none-eligible`), or whose payment the balance cannot make without it by the same exact test (reason `payment`), is recorded as infeasible for collateral, apart from build failures and from infeasibility by balance, and is not a selector failure. `evo-lf` and `evo-unfrack` have nothing set aside, since their library chooses its own collateral and cannot be given one: it selects from the whole pool, and the spend record marks the collateral as library-chosen. Their spend is infeasible for collateral (`none-eligible`) when the rule would set no UTxO aside, as for every method, since no method could build it; reason `payment` does not apply to them.
- Otherwise the method builds it from that pool, the kit checks it against the whole state, and it is applied; a failure leaves the state as it was and the replay continues. A failure of class `collateral` is a transaction whose collateral fails the kit's check, or a collateral the Evolution SDK cannot make, charged to the method whether the engine or its library chose the collateral. A transaction whose collateral is not the UTxO set aside is a defect of the method, class `other`, except under the Evolution SDK's methods. When the kit fails a transaction that carries parts its adapter wrote after the library priced it, the failure's detail ends with their names (`parts the library did not price: ...`). Every method's transaction also has its redeemers compared with the intent's, by tag and by the input, policy, reward account, certificate (its kind and credential), voter or proposal each redeems (`missingRedeemers` in `src/kit/replay.mjs`). The transaction's redeemer pointers are resolved as the history reader resolves the observed transaction's. A transaction that lacks one of the intent's redeemers fails, since the ledger rejects it (`MissingRedeemers`, `eras/alonzo/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Alonzo/Rules/Utxow.hs:251-274`, which Conway's UTXOW runs through Babbage's). The names in its detail include `redeemers`, whatever the failure's class. When the kit's checks otherwise pass, the failure is class `other`.

Collateral is the smallest UTxO eligible under the arm's rule, ties by `txid#index`, that is not one of the spend's fixed inputs and can carry, with a return output at its minimum, the total collateral of the largest fee the spend can have: a transaction of `maxTxSize` bytes with the intent's script fee. It covers any fee a transaction within the size limit reaches. The ledger allows a collateral input that is also a spent input: Conway's UTXO rule runs Babbage's, which checks the collateral UTxOs alone (`feesOK` and `validateTotalCollateral`, `eras/babbage/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Babbage/Rules/Utxo.hs:195-212` and `:241-256`), requires only that every spent, collateral and reference input be in the UTxO (`:388-390`), and keeps only the spent and reference inputs disjoint (`disjointRefInputs`, `:215-229`). The kit accepts such a collateral; only the Evolution SDK's collateral can be one, since the UTxO set aside is out of every other method's pool. A forfeit's collateral is the smallest eligible UTxO that can carry the forfeit. At every script interaction the engine records whether collateral is available under each rule. At a spend, that is whether the rule would set a UTxO aside and leave enough to make the payment, so a spend that would be infeasible for collateral counts as having none (a spend infeasible by balance needs only the UTxO). At a forfeit, it is whether any UTxO is eligible. At each parallel burst's first spend, a separate pass builds the burst's spends from the state at its start without spending any output created earlier in the burst, for burst feasibility; the sequential replay continues for fees. In the maintained-tidy arm, a plan that proposes a pure-ADA-only transaction the never-propose setting would not is an H-6 trigger, and a trigger rescues when, within 30 days of block time, the never-propose arm lacks eligible collateral at a script interaction or cannot build a parallel burst that the rule's arm can.

The window-end `conformity` of a `wallet-arm` record is computed on the end state before any restoring plan. `tokenUtxos` counts the UTxOs that are not held and carry a native asset. `conforming` counts those that break none of the treatment's output rules at the arm's planner settings (`tidy-once` and `maintained-tidy` use their own, in every variant; `observed`, `naive-tidy` and `random-tidy` the decided defaults) and the run's parameters: no fungible and non-fungible tokens together, isolation respected when on, at most `bundleSize` tokens, Value bytes at most `maxValSize`, coin equal to its minimum, no zero quantity. `conformingNoCoin` is the same count with the coin rule left out, by giving each UTxO's own coin as its minimum. Nothing is exempt: a partial split's remainder, or an output that absorbed dust change, counts as non-conforming when it breaks a rule. The verdict is the treatment's `outputViolations`, called with each UTxO's tokens, coin, minimum coin and Value bytes read from the state without CSL (`src/replay/conform.mjs`), and the class map is the treatment's `classifyAssets` over every UTxO of the state, held ones included, so the count does not depend on the planner accepting the state. A wallet with no token UTxO has no share. Beside the counts, `proposes`, `txs` and `fee` are the plan the planner would propose for the end state (the restoring plan; `fee` is its fee), `refused` carries the planner's refusal when it refuses the state (the counts are still present), and `noChangeAddress` marks a state with no change address.

H-9's cost contrast reads these records. Per wallet and seed, `intent` in a spend record names the same observed intent in every arm. The spends built in both arms, tidy-once with the layout-preserving change and maintained-tidy with the default change, contribute their `walletFee`, which is the last transaction's wallet-controlled fee and so a spend's own when it builds one transaction (`txs` is 1 on every spend `cjs-rrri` builds in these arms). The tidy-once side adds the restoring plan's fee (`conformity.fee`; a wallet and seed whose end state the planner refuses has none); the maintained-tidy side adds the `fee` of every `plan` record whose `at` is a mark (a refused mark plan carries `refused` and no fee). The `start` plan record is the same in both arms and is left out of both. Spends built in one arm only are classified by the other arm's `outcome` and `class`. Each arm's share at window end, with and without the coin rule, is read from `conformity`.

### The batteries

Which wallets run which battery follows from the history header's `split` and `sample`, decided for every file before the first job starts. A wallet in the recent random sample runs the standard battery and H-10's. A wallet in a recent purposive stratum and not in the recent random sample runs H-10's alone. Any other sampled wallet (the peak frame's samples and strata, the ADA-only samples) runs neither and starts no job. A file with no split and no `sample` (a test network's `--wallets`, a live-fire pick, a synthetic shape) runs both. A file with a split and no `sample`, or a `sample` without `recentRandom` or `recentStratum` (written before the strata were recorded), is refused, naming the file and saying to rerun `history`; nothing is written. The run header and the printed totals count the files per kind (`population`: `standard-and-h10`, `h10-only`, `none`, `no-sample`) and the wallets of the recent random sample whose snapshot state holds no native asset, held UTxOs included (`tokenless`, expected 0; counted, not filtered). No wallet is named.

The standard battery runs under every method. H-10's runs under the three baselines only (`csl-lf`, `csl-ri` and `cjs-rrri`): 95 runs per standard-battery spend and 61 per H-10 spend at 30 repetitions. The test methods run every method on every spend.

At the snapshot, from the observed layout: an ADA payment drawn from the pooled T1 amounts, one NFT, a share of one fungible drawn from the pooled T5 shares, all NFTs of one policy, p of the most spread fungible's balance for p in {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9}, and payrolls to 10 and 100 recipients; and H-10's payments of p of the ADA balance from the observed and the tidy layout. Draws come from one mulberry32 generator per wallet and spend kind, seeded from SHA-256 of the stake credential and the kind's name. A drawn ADA amount under its output's minimum is raised to that minimum, and a fungible share or payroll share that rounds to zero units pays one unit; a payroll whose wallet holds fewer units than it has recipients is left unbuilt and reported. Recipients are synthetic base addresses whose stake part is no wallet's.

"The balance suffices" (the H-12 rule's exact test) is evaluated once per wallet and spend on the layout the spend is made from, the observed layout for the standard battery and H-12, and is the infeasible-by-balance rule of every battery. Every method's answer must come within 60 seconds of wall-clock time; one that does not has returned an error with the reason "time limit", and the wallet's remaining spends continue in a fresh worker. After every spend the frozen planner plans the resulting state at its defaults. The snapshot state itself is planned once per wallet, and a wallet that runs the standard battery gets one `snapshot-plan` record of that plan ("Outputs"). When an oracle is given (`--oracle`), it is called once per wallet, standard-battery spend (H-10's payments get no call) and distinct oracle change policy, so that two selectors whose change the oracle builds the same way share one reference. The calls for a wallet run together, up to the oracle's worker count.

### Synthetic shapes and streams

The 17 shapes of the preprod seeder, S1 to S9, S2b, M1 to M5, M7 and M8 as its specification names them (byte for byte the treatment harness's), and the claim and merchant shapes CL1, CL3, E1 and E2 are generated deterministically with mulberry32. Each shape has a synthetic intent stream of fixed test values for the engine's tests, over 75 days: payments of each type T1 to T8 that its holdings allow, a deposit with a held datum UTxO, a parallel burst, a deposit at a second base address of the wallet, to which the change address moves, a Plutus spend with a script input, redeemer, script and datum, a phase-2-invalid forfeit, a payment with a validity interval, a message and a held fixed input, a self-reorganization, one with a reward withdrawal, a native-script mint, a stake registration with its deposit, and two 30-day marks.

### The fidelity check

For every observed spend that runs no script, the `fidelity` command rebuilds the transaction with the ledger kit from the spend's parts in its history file and compares two minimum fees: `minFeeA * |rebuilt| + minFeeB` and `minFeeA * |observed| + minFeeB`, both under the fee parameters of the observed transaction's epoch, which db-sync's `epoch_param` gives and every intent carries. A spend is within the tolerance when the two differ by at most 2% of the observed one (`|rebuilt - observed| * 50 <= observed`). A window passes when at least 99% of its spends are within it. The sizes need not be equal, since the kit writes its own encodings: sets with tag 258, change in the array form and synthetic vkey witnesses.

The rebuild is the replay's assembly (`assembleReplayOnce` in `src/kit/replay.mjs`) at the minimum fee found as a fixed point, as every replayed transaction is, from:

- the intent as a replay gives it to every method, without the network id and without auxiliary data holding no metadata and no script;
- the observed wallet inputs and the intent's fixed inputs;
- the requested outputs that are outputs of the observed transaction, and every other observed wallet output as change. A requested output built from an inflow is left out, since the wallet output that received the inflow already carries it;
- one synthetic vkey witness per distinct payment key of the wallet inputs, the held wallet inputs and the required signers among the wallet's payment credentials at the spend. Every other witness is kept as observed, a bootstrap witness of a non-wallet Byron input at its own size;
- no collateral.

The wallet's payment credentials at a spend are those of its UTxO set just before it, followed through the history file's events; the followed set must end at the file's end count (`stateCheck`).

A spend runs a script when it carries a Plutus redeemer or is phase-2-invalid (spend type T6), and the check leaves it out. Native scripts and reference scripts stay in: neither adds an execution term, and both fees use the same formula. Self-reorganizations are counted beside the check, since the replay does not rebuild them and a history file does not carry their fixed parts. A spend the kit cannot assemble, such as one carrying a body field 6, is counted apart with its reason and counts against its window's share; the share over the rebuilt spends is recorded beside it.

`fidelity.json` holds, per window (the history files' frame):

- the files, wallets and splits; the intents, the script spends left out and the self-reorganizations;
- the spends checked, rebuilt, within 2% and outside it, and those counted apart by reason;
- the share, the share over the rebuilt spends, and whether the share reaches 99%;
- the quantiles of the relative difference at 0, 1, 5, 25, 50, 75, 95, 99 and 100% (nearest rank), its mean and its signs, and the same quantiles of the byte difference;
- the within count and relative quantiles with the leftovers kept, which separates the replay's rule on leftovers from the kit's encodings;
- over every rebuilt spend, over those outside 2% and over those more than 1% away, counts by spend type, protocol major version, kit witnesses less observed wallet witnesses, bootstrap witnesses, native scripts, other vkey witnesses, network id left out, empty auxiliary data left out, change in the map form, and collateral fields on the observed transaction.

It holds no fee, size or identifier of a spend. For the study, the check runs once over the exploration split's history files of both windows after the draw:

```
node src/cli.mjs fidelity --treatment <dir> --history <run directory>/history --run-id <id>
```

A confirmation-split file is refused unless `--analysis-freeze-tx` names the transaction whose record freezes the analysis scripts.

cardano-serialization-lib (CSL) is the treatment's instance, loaded through its `cslkit.mjs`; no other CSL build is installed. Every adapter receives the spendable UTxOs in transaction id then index order, runs its library with the run's protocol parameters, and maps the library's errors to the failure classes. The engine runs the ledger kit's checks on what it returns; no adapter checks, retries or repairs its library's output. Each adapter's source comments cite the library code it relies on, by repository, commit, file and line.

Serialization and witnesses. A library that returns a transaction keeps its own encoding. CSL's witness set is filled with one synthetic vkey witness per distinct payment key among the inputs (the treatment's `build.mjs` witnesses), and the Evolution SDK's transaction is taken with its own fake witnesses, one per payment key. cardano-js-sdk selects inputs and computes change only, so the kit assembles its transaction, and its four constraint callbacks are computed with the kit on the transaction as it will be assembled, at the wallet's change address:

- `computeMinimumCost`: the ledger's minimum fee for the skeleton's transaction, its fee field at that fee;
- `computeMinimumCoinQuantity`: CSL's `min_ada_for_output` for the output;
- `tokenBundleSizeExceedsLimit`: the bundle's Value, its coin at the widest encoding, against `maxValSize` (the library passes the bundle without its coin; setting `cjsBundleCoin` in `src/settings.mjs`);
- `computeSelectionLimit`: the skeleton's input count when its transaction fits `maxTxSize`, one fewer when it does not.

Change substitution. With a change policy other than `default` (`layout`, `single` or `naive`), `csl-lf`, `csl-ri`, `csl-lf-pure`, `csl-ri-pure` and `cjs-rrri` keep only the inputs their library chose (one code path in `src/methods/csl.mjs` for the four CSL methods, and `src/methods/cjs.mjs`), and the simulator's policy (`src/methods/change.mjs`) builds the change, the kit assembling the transaction at its exact fee. When those inputs cannot fund the change, the spend fails with class `change-minimum`. `evo-lf` takes no substitution: under another change policy it fails with class `other` (`src/methods/evo.mjs`).

Replay path. A replayed intent can carry parts a battery spend never does: fixed parts (inputs the selector does not choose, reference inputs, certificates, withdrawals, the mint field, validity, required signers, votes, proposals, the treasury fields, auxiliary data and witnesses), collateral for a script spend, implicit values (withdrawals, refunds, deposits, a donation, a mint or a burn), and requested outputs that carry a datum or a reference script. Each adapter states which of these parts its replay path covers (`REPLAY_COVERAGE` in `src/methods/library.mjs`), and before it builds anything it refuses an intent with a part it does not cover (`refuseUncovered`). `cjs-rrri`, the four CSL methods and `evo-lf` cover every part; `evo-lf`'s collateral is its library's own choice, since the library cannot be given the UTxO the engine would set aside (below). The survey's Mesh adapter covers none, since the survey runs battery spends only. A refusal is an ordinary error, so the engine records it as a failed spend of class `other` with its message, never as not replayable (`src/replay/engine.mjs`). The batteries' spends carry none of these parts, so every baseline runs them.

`cjs-rrri` passes each part through the library's own parameters for what a selection has to balance, and through the ledger kit for the rest:

| Part | How `cjs-rrri` replays it |
|---|---|
| Fixed inputs: other parties' and script inputs, and the wallet's held UTxOs the spend consumes | `preSelectedUtxo`. The selection starts from them, their value counts toward every target, and their tokens that no output requests go to the change. They are never offered as ordinary UTxOs. |
| Withdrawals, refunds, deposits | `implicitValue.coin`: `withdrawals`, refunds as `reclaimDeposit`, withdrawals and refunds summed as `input`, and deposits as `deposit` |
| A treasury donation | added to `implicitValue.coin.deposit`, which the library counts as coin spent outside the outputs. The library has no donation field. |
| A mint or a burn | `implicitValue.mint`, a burn as a negative quantity |
| Requested outputs | `outputs`, by address and value. The kit writes each as its own bytes, so a datum, a reference script and a coin raised to its minimum pass through as observed. |
| Every other fixed part: reference inputs, certificates, the withdrawal and mint fields, validity, required signers, votes, proposals, the treasury fields, auxiliary data, scripts, datums and redeemers | The library has no parameter for these; the values of the withdrawal and mint fields reach it through `implicitValue`. The kit writes each as its original bytes into every transaction the callbacks assemble and into the final one, so `computeMinimumCost` and `computeSelectionLimit` count their bytes. `computeMinimumCost` adds the intent's script fee, computed from the observed execution units. |
| Collateral | The UTxO the engine set aside under the arm's rule before selection (`setAside` in `src/replay/engine.mjs`), which is not among the UTxOs the library is given. Every transaction the callbacks assemble, and the final one, carries it with a return output and the total-collateral field. A script spend with no UTxO to set aside, or that cannot be paid without it, is infeasible for collateral and never reaches the library. |

An intent the kit cannot write, one with body field 6 (protocol parameter updates, which no Conway transaction carries), is not replayable for `cjs-rrri` and is counted apart from build failures. Every replayed intent's transaction is assembled by `src/kit/replay.mjs`, even one with none of these parts, so its requested outputs keep their bytes. A battery spend's transaction is assembled by `src/kit/assemble.mjs`.

The four CSL methods build every replayed intent through CSL's own builders at 13.2.1 (`rust/src/builders/` at commit 846d0f2), its fixed parts first, since `add_inputs_from` expects them in place. Then `add_inputs_from` runs over the pool, then `add_change_if_needed` and `build_tx`, as for a battery spend:

| Part | How the CSL methods replay it |
|---|---|
| Requested outputs | `add_output` with each output's own bytes, so a datum, a reference script and a coin raised to its minimum pass through |
| Fixed inputs: other parties' and script inputs, and the wallet's held UTxOs the spend consumes | One `TxInputsBuilder` given by `set_inputs`, which `add_inputs_from` extends. A key or Byron input goes in by `add_regular_input`, a native script input by `add_native_script_input` with its script. | A mint or a burn | The mint builder, with each policy's native script, or its Plutus script and redeemer |
| Certificates, with their deposits and refunds | The certificates builder, with a script witness where a certificate needs one. CSL computes the deposits and refunds from the certificates. |
| Withdrawals | The withdrawals builder, in the ledger's order of reward accounts, which the history reader's redeemer targets follow too (`compareRewardAccounts` in `src/history/tx.mjs`) |
| Votes and proposals | The voting and proposal builders, with their script witnesses |

| Validity, required signers, auxiliary data, the treasury value and a donation | The transaction builder's setters. CSL writes the auxiliary data in its own encoding. |
| Network id | None. A replayed intent carries none, and the transaction builder writes none. |

An intent with a part that CSL has no way to express is not replayable for the CSL methods and is counted apart from build failures. Such parts are body field 6; a deposit or refund that CSL counts differently from the transaction (CSL charges a pool deposit for every pool registration); a script, datum or redeemer that a part needs and the intent does not hold; and a part that CSL refuses, or a script that CSL reads under another hash. An intent whose witness set holds a script or a redeemer that no part CSL builds takes is not replayable either. Under a substituted change policy, the simulator's policy receives only the inputs `add_inputs_from` chose, not the fixed inputs, and the kit assembles the transaction with the fixed parts and the collateral.

`evo-lf` passes each part through the Evolution SDK's own operations where it has one, and its execution units through the build's evaluator:

| Part | How `evo-lf` replays it |
|---|---|
| Fixed inputs: other parties' and script inputs, and the wallet's held UTxOs the spend consumes | `collectFrom`, one input per call with that input's redeemer. Fixed inputs are never among the UTxOs the library may select. |
| Reference inputs | `readFrom` |
| A mint or a burn | `mintAssets` per policy, a burn as a negative quantity, with the policy's redeemer |

| Refunds and deposits | The library computes them from the certificates and proposals above, except for a legacy registration or deregistration, for which it counts none. |

A part the library's builder can neither write nor price goes on the transaction the library built, through the library's own types, so the replayed transaction carries every part of the replayed intent. These parts are the treasury fields (body fields 21 and 22), auxiliary data carrying scripts, which `attachMetadata` cannot write, a witness-set datum that no spent input needs, such as an output's, which `collectFrom` refuses (written with every observed datum and the script-data hash from the library's own function), bootstrap witnesses, and the certificates or withdrawals when one belongs to a script stake credential with no redeemer, which the library's stake operations refuse. The library's fee does not count such a part, and a donation or deposit it did not balance stays unbalanced, so the kit fails the spend with class `fee-minimum` or `balance` and the failure's detail names the part. No operation writes a network id or an auxiliary data without metadata or scripts either, and a replayed intent carries neither. Only a part the library's types cannot hold makes the intent not replayable for `evo-lf`: body field 6, as for `cjs-rrri`, a Byron address on a fixed or reference input, and a genesis-delegation or MIR certificate. The adapter, given a network id or an empty auxiliary data directly, finds them not replayable too. The transaction has the library's default encoding, since no `codecOptions` is given: the library writes redeemer data in its default Plutus data encoding, and writes a datum given through `collectFrom` with its observed bytes. The library computes a script-data hash only from full protocol parameters, over the cost models they hold. The simulator holds none, so the library hashes the redeemers with an empty language view, where the kit's replay assembly hashes the redeemers and datums alone. Both hashes are 32 bytes, and no check reads their value.

### Builder survey

`src/survey/` holds the library part of the builder survey. Its fixture (`fixture.mjs`) is a wallet of k UTxOs, k from 1 to 5, each with 141 one-unit tokens of its own test policy under 32-byte names (a 4,974-byte Value) and 100 ADA. ## Oracle

The oracle computes the reference a selector's fee is measured against. For one wallet state, one battery spend, the run's protocol parameters and one change policy, it searches for the least fee any input set reaches in a single transaction under that policy, within a fixed work budget, and reports that fee with its status. The fee is an upper bound on the optimum, and an `optimal` status proves it equal over the status's scope. There is no oracle for multi-transaction plans. The code is in `src/oracle/`.

It takes the standard battery's and H-10's spends, which carry requested outputs only, so it handles no script, certificate, collateral, implicit value or fixed part. A spend with any of those, with no requested output, from another source, or with a requested output that carries a datum or a reference script is refused with an `OracleRefusal`, as is an unknown change policy. Held UTxOs are never candidates. An input set is feasible under a policy when the policy's own code, given exactly that set as the transaction's inputs, builds a transaction that passes every check of the ledger kit; every evaluation is cached by its set.

```
const o = createOracle({ treatmentDir, pinsText, workers })
const r = await o.oracle(state, spend, params, policy, { incumbents, settings })
await o.close()
```

`createOracle` (`src/oracle/oracle.mjs`) needs the treatment's directory; `pinsText`, a digest list's text, defaults to that of `data/treatment-pins.txt`, and `workers` to 1. Each worker thread loads the treatment once, after checking its digests, and runs one call at a time; further calls wait for a free worker. `incumbents` lists the methods' transactions as `{ name, seeded, walletInputs, bytes, fee }`, and `settings` is a result of `oracleSettings` (the defaults when absent). The checks also pass `path` (`exhaustive` or `mip`) and `mipSpace` (`candidates`) to force one path over one search space, and, under the setting `limit=wall-clock` only, `timeLimitMs` (60,000 by default); a call given `timeLimitMs` under the work budget is refused.

The battery runner (`runBatteries` in `src/replay/run.mjs`) takes an oracle as a hook of two functions: `policyOf(methodName, changePolicy)`, the oracle's policy for a method's change or null, and `reference(state, intent, params, policy, { incumbents })`. It calls `reference` once per wallet, observed-layout spend and distinct oracle policy, with the single transaction of every run under that policy that passed the kit, by a method the setting `incumbents` admits, as an incumbent (each repetition and option of each seeded method, under the default; a run that answered with a chain, timed out or failed adds none), and writes the result as a `battery` record whose method is `oracle` and whose change is the policy. The passing runs of the methods it does not admit never seed the search; the record keeps them beside the reference as `unseeded`, per method: the runs, how many have a fee below the reference's (every one when the reference has none), and the least fee. The battery's totals count the references the guard stopped (`oracleGuard`). `policyOfMethod` (`src/oracle/policies.mjs`) maps `csl-lf` and `csl-ri` to `csl`, `csl-lf-pure` and `csl-ri-pure` to `csl-ppc`, `cjs-rrri` to `cjs`, `evo-lf` to `evo`, and the H-12 rule to `layout`. The `battery` command builds the hook (`createBatteryOracle`, `src/oracle/battery-hook.mjs`) when given `--oracle`, with `--oracle-workers` and `--oracle-settings`, and passes none otherwise, in which case its runs write no oracle records. The hook's `policyOf` returns only the policies this build can evaluate, so a method whose change the build cannot evaluate gets no reference. A spend the oracle refuses is recorded with the status `refused` and its reason.

Under `--methods study` the calls reach five policies, with these incumbents: `csl` (`csl-lf` once), `csl-ppc` (`csl-lf-pure` once), `cjs` (`cjs-rrri`, one per repetition, seeds 1 to the repetition count), `evo` (`evo-lf` once, absent where its answer reached the answer limit) and `layout` (the H-12 rule with absorption on and off). `csl-ri` and `csl-ri-pure` draw from a generator that cannot be seeded, so their runs, once per repetition, are kept beside the `csl` and `csl-ppc` references and never seed them; a reference can therefore be above an unseeded run's fee, and the record says how many are. The oracle check's methods (`test/lib/oracle-methods.mjs`) differ: `csl-ri` and `csl-ri-pure` once each, kept apart in the same way, `cjs-rrri` at seeds 1 to 3, and `layout` with the test method added, all built directly with no answer limit.

The `oracle` field of that record holds the status, the path or paths that ran and `bestFrom` (the incumbent, the exhaustive search or the program that produced the reference), the scope, the candidate count, the size of the candidate set, the fee and the bound, whether the reference lies in the search space and in the candidate set, the reference transaction's input count, size, fee and minimum fee, `stop` (the limit that ended the call), `work` (the work it used) and the program's bound, whether the call was terminated, each incumbent's outcome under the policy (its fee, the oracle's fee for its input set, and whether its change equals the policy's byte for byte), the evaluation counts, the settings and the solver. It holds neither the transaction's bytes nor its hash nor its input references, which the record guard refuses, and its time goes to `timings.jsonl` like any other.

### Change policies and the fee

| Policy | Change of | How a set is built |
|---|---|---|
| `csl` | `csl-lf`, `csl-ri` | cardano-serialization-lib 13.2.1's `TransactionBuilder` configured from the run's parameters: the set added with `add_regular_input` at each UTxO's own address, the requested outputs from their bytes, then `add_change_if_needed` at the change address and `build_tx`; the library keeps its body and fee, and the witness set takes the treatment's synthetic witnesses |
| `csl-ppc` | `csl-lf-pure`, `csl-ri-pure` | the `csl` policy with `prefer_pure_change` set |
| `cjs` | `cjs-rrri` | `roundRobinRandomImprove` with the set as `preSelectedUtxo` and no other UTxO available, so that the library's own `computeChangeBundles` makes the change from the set alone, under the adapter's constraint callbacks; with nothing to draw, its random source never chooses; the kit assembles the transaction at the library's fee |
| `evo` | `evo-lf` | the Evolution SDK's builder, given the set through `collectFrom` in the order its largest-first selection holds (lovelace descending, then `txid#index`), since it writes a change output's assets in the order its inputs brought them, and no available UTxOs; the transaction is the library's own, with its own fake witnesses |
| `layout` | the H-12 rule, and any selector under the `layout` change policy | the simulator's layout-preserving change (`src/methods/change.mjs`), assembled by the kit at the ledger minimum |

A method under the `single` or `naive` change policy has no oracle policy. `cjs` and `evo` reach their libraries through the baseline adapters' modules, loaded when first needed, and are refused when those cannot be loaded. A library that selects inputs other than the forced set fails the call with an error.

The fee a feasible set is scored by is the setting `setFee`. At its default, `as-built`, it is the fee the transaction pays, since that is the user's cost: the ledger minimum under `layout`, and the library's own fee under the other four. cardano-serialization-lib's fee is above the ledger minimum, since its estimate counts the validity flag and a 9-byte fee field, and the library burns an ADA-only leftover below the change minimum into the fee; that burn is part of the score. A library fee under the ledger minimum fails the kit's fee check, so its set is not feasible. Under `ledger-minimum`, a set is scored at `minFeeA` times its transaction's ledger size plus `minFeeB`, whatever fee the transaction carries, and the oracle can then choose a transaction that burns its leftover at a score far below what it pays.

### The search

The candidate set is the spendable UTxOs holding any requested asset, and the 20 largest spendable pure-ADA UTxOs by coin, ties by `txid#index`. When it numbers at most 24, the run takes the exhaustive path over it; otherwise the mixed-integer program over every spendable UTxO.

Both paths rest on a lower bound (`src/oracle/bound.mjs`) on the fee of every feasible transaction from a set, under any policy. It counts only the bytes every such transaction holds, each in its shortest CBOR encoding under the Conway CDDL: the transaction and body headers; each input, 36 to 38 bytes by its index; the requested outputs as given; the fee field, holding at least `minFeeB`; the witness set with one 101-byte vkey witness per distinct key payment credential; the auxiliary data's null; and the change. When the inputs hold a token the requested outputs do not take whole, the change is at least one output at the change address holding every returned token entry, its coin at least that output's minimum; otherwise it is an ADA-only output or none, the fee then taking the whole leftover as cardano-serialization-lib does (under `ledger-minimum`, only the bytes without a change output count). The fee is at least `minFeeA` times those bytes plus `minFeeB`. A set that lacks a requested asset, whose coin cannot cover the requested coin, that fee and the change minimum, or whose bytes pass `maxTxSize`, has no feasible transaction under any policy.

The exhaustive path is a best-first branch and bound over the candidate set's subsets, each node's children adding one candidate after its last. A node is queued by the bound of its subtree: its own fee bound plus `minFeeA` times 36 bytes, the shortest input, for each input the subtree must still add. That count is the least number of remaining candidates, largest first, whose quantities reach a requested asset's shortfall or whose effective values (coin less the input's own fee, as in Erhardt's Branch and Bound) reach the coin shortfall, whichever is larger. A node whose remaining candidates cannot cover a shortfall, or whose bytes would pass the size limit, is pruned; one that meets every necessary condition is evaluated exactly when it leaves the queue. The search stops at the first node whose bound is at or above the best fee: every set not yet evaluated lies under a queued node, so the best is the optimum over the candidate set.

The mixed-integer program (`src/oracle/mip.mjs`) relaxes the same bound over a pool of UTxOs: a binary selection variable per UTxO; coverage of every requested asset; the returned token entries, a policy entry per returned policy and a witness per payment key; the bytes at most `maxTxSize`; the fee, its objective, at least `minFeeA` times the bytes plus `minFeeB`; the coin covering the requested coin, the fee and the change minimums; and at least one input. On a pool of at most 40 UTxOs a requested asset's return and the change output are binary and the no-change branch is kept; on a larger pool they are continuous and the branch is dropped, a weaker bound that spares the solver exact-cover problems. Every term is at most what the exact transaction holds, so the program's optimum bounds the fee of every set it has not excluded.

Every set the program chooses is checked against the bound's exact conditions, and a set that meets them is rebuilt exactly by the policy and checked by the kit; every rejection is recorded with its reason. A feasible set cheaper than the best becomes the best, and the program's cutoff falls to one lovelace under its fee. The chosen set is then excluded by a no-good cut and the program solved again, until it is infeasible (no remaining set beats the best, which is then the optimum over the pool, or there is none) or the budget is spent. Each solve is capped at `maxSolveNodes` branch-and-bound nodes, so that a slow proof still leaves work to rebuild other sets, and yields the best set it holds at the cap; a capped solve that finds no set is followed by solves that may take every node the call has left. The program's bound is the highest dual bound any solve reported, rounded down to the lovelace; cuts and a falling cutoff only shrink the program, so the optimum over the pool is at least the lower of the best fee and that bound.

A run first evaluates its incumbents, each method's input set rebuilt by the policy, and starts from the cheapest feasible one, so that the reference is never above the policy's fee on a method's own inputs. It then takes its path. Under the default `afterExhaustive` value, `mip-always`, an exhaustive search that completes is followed by the program over every spendable UTxO for the rest of the budget, its cutoff starting from the best found so far, and the lower fee is kept, so that a spend the candidate set cannot build is not reported infeasible. When the candidate set holds every spendable UTxO, a completed exhaustive search already covers the pool and no program follows it, under any value of the setting. No program follows an exhaustive search that stops at its budget.

### The budget and the guard

A call stops on a fixed work budget (the setting `limit`, `budget` by default), counted on one thread with a fixed solver seed, so that its result is the same for the same state, spend, parameters, policy, incumbents and settings whatever the machine's load and whichever worker runs it:

- `maxCandidateSets`: the candidate sets the call evaluates exactly, the exhaustive search's evaluations and the program's rebuilt sets together, so that a program after a completed exhaustive search takes the rest. The exhaustive search checks it before each evaluation, so a search that completes within the budget is never cut short; the program checks it before each rebuild.
- `maxSolves`: the program's solves.
- `maxNodes`: the branch-and-bound nodes of all the program's solves together.
- `maxSolveNodes`: the most nodes one solve takes (HiGHS's `mip_max_nodes`) before it yields its best set, unless a capped solve has found no set.

The incumbents are evaluated apart and not counted, so a reference's search does not grow with the number of runs that seed it. No solve has a time limit. The budget's values are recorded in every result's `settings`; `work` reports what the call used, under the same names (`candidateSets`, `solves`, `nodes`) with the most nodes of one solve (`mostSolveNodes`), the simplex iterations (`lpIterations`), the incumbent sets evaluated (`incumbentSets`) and the exhaustive queue's pops (`queuePops`). `stop` names what ended the call: `proven` (the last search completed), `candidate-sets`, `solves` or `nodes` (that part of the budget), `solver-error`, `status` or `empty` (the program stopped early, as below), `wall-clock` (under the wall-clock limit) or `guard`.

The guard is a wall-clock backstop, the setting `guardMs`, set well above any budgeted call. CSL's and HiGHS's calls are synchronous and cannot be interrupted from inside, so the parent terminates a worker whose call has not returned by then, records the termination and starts a fresh worker. A call it stops is `undecided`, with no fee, `terminated` true, `stop` `guard`, no `work`, and no path, scope or bound, whatever the worker had found, since how far it got depends on the load.

The other value, `limit=wall-clock`, is a time limit of 60 seconds of wall-clock time per call (`timeLimitMs`): the search checks its deadline between evaluations and between solves, each solve is capped at a tenth of the limit (at least one second), and a capped solve that finds no set is followed by one with all the time left. The parent then terminates a worker 5 seconds after the limit (`createOracle`'s `marginMs`). Its work is counted the same way. It is kept for the oracle's checks, to measure the work a time limit allows, and the `battery` command refuses it.

The solver is HiGHS, the npm package `highs` at exactly 1.15.4 (HiGHS compiled to WebAssembly, MIT, `github.com/lovasoa/highs-js`); the oracle refuses any other installed version. The WebAssembly build is single-threaded and refuses the threads and parallel options. Each solve runs with a relative gap of zero and HiGHS's `random_seed` set to the setting `solverSeed` (0). HiGHS reports a solve stopped at its node limit with the status `solutionLimit`; such a solve yields its best set, if it holds one, as a solve stopped at its time limit does. HiGHS 1.15.4's presolve aborts on some of these programs once enough cuts have been added, with a WebAssembly runtime error that leaves the solver instance unusable; it never does with presolve off. On any solver failure the loop takes a fresh instance, rebuilds the program with every cut and the cutoff, and goes on with presolve off for the rest of the run; a second failure ends the run unproven. Every failure is recorded. An instance an earlier call left aborted is replaced at the start of the next call and recorded as inherited, without counting as one of that call's failures.

### Statuses

Each run returns one status, from the last search that ran:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `optimal` | the search completed with a feasible set: the exhaustive queue ran out at a bound at or above the best fee, or the program under its cutoff became infeasible; the fee is the optimum over the scope, unless an incumbent from outside the scope is lower |
| `best-at-limit` | a feasible set exists, and the search stopped before it completed: at a limit of its budget, or when the program stopped early for a reason its stop names, such as a second solver failure |
| `infeasible` | the search completed without a feasible set: the scope holds none |
| `undecided` | the search stopped before it completed, with no feasible set |

`scope` names the UTxOs a status speaks for. It is `spendable` when the program ran over every spendable UTxO, or when the candidate set is the whole pool and the exhaustive search covered it. It is `candidates` when the search covered the candidate set only: an exhaustive search that stopped at its budget, one that no program followed under `report` (or under `mip`, when it found a set), or the program forced onto the candidates. Under the default, a run is therefore never `optimal` or `infeasible` over the candidate set alone. `bound` is a lower bound on the optimum over the scope: the fee when `optimal`, the lower of the fee and the program's bound when the program stopped unproven, the program's bound when the program ended `undecided`, and null when nothing bounds it, as after an exhaustive search that stopped at its budget.

A result also holds:

- `fee` and `tx`, the reference transaction's bytes, both null without a feasible set, and `best`: its scored fee, the fee it carries, the ledger minimum, its size, witness count and inputs;
- `bestFrom`, what produced the reference (`incumbent`, `exhaustive` or `mip`), and `bestInCandidates`, whether its inputs lie in the candidate set;
- `path`, the path taken, `paths`, the searches that ran, `candidates`, the number of UTxOs the last search covered, and `candidateSet`, the candidate set's size;
- `exhaustive`: the exhaustive search's own result over the candidates (whether it completed, its status and fee, and whether the candidate set is the whole pool);
- `incumbents`: each one's result (feasible, a failure class, or `not-in-state` when an input is held or absent) and whether its change equals the policy's on its inputs, byte for byte;
- `stop` and `work`, the limit that ended the call and the work it used (above);
- `detail`: the exhaustive search's node counts and its stop, and, for the program, its stop, its bound, its solves, rebuilt and refuted sets, nodes and simplex iterations, its rejections and its solver failures;
- `evaluations`, `settings`, `solver` and `ms`, and from the parent `wallMs` and `terminated`.

## Hashing

`npm run hash` prints the SHA-256 of every file under `src/`, `sql/` and `data/` and of `package.json` and `package-lock.json`, the pinned dependencies with their npm integrity, and the Node version, in the format of the extraction tool's code manifest. ## Tests

`npm test` runs the unit tests: no database and no chain data, and without the treatment the tests described below as needing it are skipped. They cover CBOR spans and encoders, BLAKE2b against Node's 64-byte digest and known 32-byte digests, CIP-19's address vectors, Base58, CIP-67's ten test vectors and the five registry prefixes, the classification rule, value sizes against the ledger encoding over random values, both output forms with datums and reference scripts, transactions of both era layouts with their size rule, redeemers in both forms, reward accounts in the ledger's order, the ImmutableDB format on a constructed chunk (including a CRC-32 mismatch), parameters and both fee terms, bursts (a chained pair in one block, 59 and 61 seconds apart, a parallel burst), marks, the observed replay, spend types, asset classes, settings and the code manifest, the thread cap's refusals in `replay`, `battery` and `study`, the battery's refusal of `--oracle-limit-ms` and of the wall-clock limit, the unseeded runs kept beside a reference, and the oracle's parts that need no treatment: its settings and their refusals, the incumbents rule, the search's refusal of an unseeded incumbent and of a time limit under the budget, HiGHS 1.15.4 stopping a solve at its node limit with status `solutionLimit` and the same work for the same program and seed, the spends it refuses, the record made of an oracle result (which the record guard accepts and which carries no transaction), the checks' default output directory, input sizes by index, the least count of inputs that reaches a shortfall, the method-to-policy mapping, when the program follows an exhaustive search, and its statuses and bounds.

`test/unit/core/battery-population.test.mjs` checks, on each header case (recent random sample, recent stratum only, peak random only, in no sample, no split and no sample, a split with a null `sample`, a `sample` without `recentStratum`), which batteries a file runs and which are refused; that a refused file refuses the set; the counts; and the runs per spend (95 on the standard battery, 61 on H-10's, every method on every spend under the test methods). `test/treatment/battery-population.test.mjs` needs the treatment: it runs the battery over constructed snapshot files, one per case, and checks each file's records against its batteries and methods, the oracle's calls on standard-battery spends only, the `snapshot-plan` records, the counts and the token-holding check, and that a refused file stops the command before any record is written.

`test/unit/core/library-replay.test.mjs` checks each library adapter's statement of the replay parts it covers, and that an adapter covering none refuses each part with the message for that part. With the treatment, it replays by `cjs-rrri` the synthetic streams of S1, S7, S8, M1 and M7 under the ledger and token-bearing collateral rules and every change option. It also replays a constructed burn, a deregistration with its refund and a reward withdrawal, a treasury donation and a body field 6. No spend fails with class `other`, and the body field 6 is not replayable. Every built transaction passes the kit's checks, with its fixed inputs present and none of them among the selector's, and its four negative controls are caught. At the default change, the library's own fee equals the final transaction's minimum.

`test/unit/core/library-replay-csl.test.mjs` needs the treatment. It replays by the four CSL methods, at the default change and under the `layout` policy, the synthetic streams of S1, S7, S8, M1 and M7 under the ledger and token-bearing collateral rules. It also replays constructed intents: a Plutus spend with its datum and redeemer, a Plutus mint whose script is a reference script, a burn, a deregistration with its refund and a reward withdrawal, a treasury donation, a payment carrying a network id and an empty auxiliary data, a deposit that CSL counts differently, a body field 6, and a Shelley stake registration carrying its stake key's witness. No spend fails with class `other`. The engine leaves the network id and the empty auxiliary data out of the payment, which builds without them. Under `csl-lf` and `csl-lf-pure` the Shelley registration fails with class `fee-minimum`, its detail naming the observed vkey witnesses CSL did not count. A `csl-ri` or `csl-ri-pure` draw can instead fail it with class `change-minimum`, or build it: CSL then burns an ADA-only leftover below the change minimum into the fee, so the transaction has no output and a fee that covers the witness. Whenever CSL builds it, the registration's observed witness is the only part CSL did not price. The streams' Plutus spend names a script whose hash its input's address does not carry, so it is not replayable for CSL, as are the constructed deposit and body field 6. Every built transaction passes the kit's checks and its negative controls are caught. In every built transaction the fixed inputs are present and none of them is among the selector's, and a script spend's only collateral is the UTxO set aside, never an input. CSL's fee covers the final transaction's minimum wherever every part was written before CSL priced it, and the script data hash is BLAKE2b-256 over the redeemers and datums as written. `csl-ri` draws from a generator no caller can seed, so its outcomes are asserted, not its bytes.

`test/unit/core/library-replay-evo.test.mjs` checks that `evo-lf` covers every part, its collateral as its library chooses it, and that only the Evolution SDK's methods may choose their own collateral. With the treatment, it replays by `evo-lf` the synthetic streams of S1, S7, S8, M1 and M7 under the ledger and token-bearing collateral rules, and a constructed burn, Conway registration, delegation and deregistration with a reward withdrawal, a payment with an inline-datum output, a required signer and a validity interval, a legacy deregistration, a treasury donation, payments carrying a network id or an empty auxiliary data, and a body field 6. Every built transaction passes the kit's checks, with its fixed inputs present and none of them among the selector's, and its four negative controls are caught; a script spend's collateral is recorded as library-chosen; the library's fee is never under the final transaction's minimum unless a part was written after it priced the transaction. No spend replayed by `evo-lf` fails with class `other`. The streams' script spends build: their script input holds a datum hash, and the library takes the datum through `collectFrom`, writes it with its observed bytes and prices it. A legacy registration or deregistration fails with class `balance`, and the body field 6 is not replayable. The payments carrying a network id or an empty auxiliary data build without them, and the adapter given them directly finds them not replayable. A witness-set datum no spent input needs, a donation, a script stake credential's registration without a redeemer, and the cold key's witness on a committee hot-key authorization are each on the library's transaction, which fails with class `fee-minimum` or `balance`, its detail naming the part. A constructed script spend builds with the library's own collateral from the whole pool: the first of the largest pure-ADA UTxOs, a total of 5 ADA, which the transaction also spends. With one UTxO of 20 ADA the library spends it as input and collateral, where the test method is infeasible for collateral (`payment`); with no UTxO the rule would set aside, both are infeasible for collateral (`none-eligible`); a collateral the library cannot make fails with class `collateral`; and under any other method's name the library's collateral is refused as class `other`. A mint and a zero-amount script withdrawal whose redeemers are the integer 0 build, each with its redeemer. Built by the adapter alone, without the kit's checks, the streams' datum-hash spend has nothing unpriced and a fee at least the final minimum. Given a second datum that no input needs, the same spend comes back with both datums, that one named unpriced and the library's fee under the final minimum.

`test/unit/core/replay-redeemers.test.mjs` needs the treatment. `npm run test:oracle` runs the oracle's checks, one file at a time, on the treatment `RESEARCH_SIM_TREATMENT_DIR` names, with `RESEARCH_SIM_TREATMENT_PINS` naming a digest list other than `data/treatment-pins.txt`; without `RESEARCH_SIM_TREATMENT_DIR` they are skipped. The brute-force and battery checks take every standard battery spend the battery does not leave unbuilt, under every policy this build evaluates:

- `brute.test.mjs`, on the shapes with at most 12 spendable UTxOs (S1, S2b, S8, M1 and M7) and on two sub-wallets of 8 to 12 UTxOs drawn with mulberry32 from each of the other 16 shapes (seeds 4001 to 4032), every subset of the spendable UTxOs evaluated exactly: the oracle's status is proven unless a limit of its work budget stopped it (such runs are counted and listed: a budget sized on the heaviest shapes can stop a small program whose proof takes many cheap solves), its scope names the space its `afterExhaustive` value gives (every spendable UTxO on the program path, when the candidate set is the whole pool, under `mip-always`, and under `mip` when no candidate subset builds; the candidate set otherwise), and its fee is the least exact fee over that space, or it is `infeasible` exactly when no subset there builds; a stopped run's fee is that least fee (none where no subset builds), a higher one, or none; the bound is never above a feasible set's exact fee, and no set it calls infeasible builds; and the program forced over the candidates, and over every spendable UTxO, gives the least exact fee over that space, or a higher fee or none only at the limit;
- `battery.test.mjs`, on the 21 shapes, each spend run seeded with the seeded methods' transactions and unseeded: no call fails; every reference transaction passes the kit, and each of the four negative controls is caught by its own check; where the exhaustive path ran, the program forced over the same candidates gives the exhaustive search's own fee, or, when that search completed, a higher fee or none only at the limit; the seeded reference fee is never above the fee of a seeded method under the same policy whose transaction passed the kit, and never missing where one passed; and under `mip-always` no run is `optimal` or `infeasible` over the candidate set alone. It fails on any difference between a method's change and the policy's on the method's own inputs, byte for byte, the unseeded methods' included. It also reports per policy the statuses with their scopes, the stops, the guard stops, the runs at the limit, the run times, and the unseeded methods' passing runs whose fee is below the reference's. The incumbents are, for `layout`, the H-12 rule with absorption on and off and the test method; for `csl`, `csl-lf`; for `csl-ppc`, `csl-lf-pure`; for `cjs`, `cjs-rrri` at seeds 1 to 3; and for `evo`, `evo-lf`. `csl-ri` and `csl-ri-pure` are built once each and kept apart. Each record holds every field but its times as a function of the shapes, the policies and the settings, except the unseeded methods' answers (`unseededMethods`);
- `limits.test.mjs`, on E1's fungible share (400 holders of one fungible) and S4's 100-recipient payroll under `csl`, whose programs run far past small budgets: a budget of 4 candidate sets stops the call `best-at-limit` with `stop` `candidate-sets` and 4 sets used, and the same call in two workers at once and again in a worker that has run another call gives the same record but for its times; a node budget of 600 with 150 per solve stops the call at 600 nodes with a solve ended at its node limit, the same in two workers, and a budget of 3 solves stops it at 3; a guard of 3 seconds terminates the call into `undecided` with `stop` `guard` and no fee, and a fresh worker then answers S8's ADA payment `optimal` over every spendable UTxO; under `limit=wall-clock`, a 5-second call returns at its limit with its work counted, and a margin of minus 2 seconds terminates the worker 3 seconds in.

For the brute-force and battery checks, `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_WORKERS` sets the worker count (4, at most 12, the commands' cap), `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_SHAPES` and `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_POLICIES` narrow a run to the shapes and policies they list, comma-separated, and `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_SETTINGS` sets the oracle's settings as comma-separated `<name>=<value>` pairs (`afterExhaustive=report` or `maxSolves=50`, for example). With `limit=wall-clock` among them, `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_LIMIT_MS` sets each call's time limit (60,000); under the work budget there is none, and the variable is refused. Those two checks write their records as JSON Lines, `oracle-brute.jsonl` and `oracle-battery.jsonl`, to the directory `RESEARCH_SIM_ORACLE_OUT` names, or else to `synthetic/oracle-checks-<time>/` under the simulator's private root (`RESEARCH_SIM_HOME`, or `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-$HOME/.local/share}/unfrackit-research/sim`), directories 0700 and files 0600.

`npm run test:preprod` runs the integration tests against a preprod database, as of one snapshot block (`RESEARCH_SIM_SNAPSHOT`), since db-sync keeps following the chain while they run; their environment is described in `test/integration/history/env.mjs`. They cover:

- the reconstruction against the reference query, for the seeded wallets and 1,000 random wallets at 20 blocks;
- outputs re-encoded from db-sync's columns against their original bytes, by output form;
- the 17 seeded shapes against the preprod seeder's specification (`tools/preprod-seeder`, used through its `address` command and its deterministic generator only), and the shapes' own spends as intents; a UTxO whose creating transaction spends no input of the funder or of a shape, sent by someone else, is left out of the comparison and reported;
- every spend of the seeder's funder and of 200 further wallets over the last 60 days, with the burst cases they contain, each file read back by the simulator's reader with every record's creating transaction id checked against db-sync's;
- phase-2-invalid transactions;
- the ImmutableDB against `tx_cbor`, byte for byte, across every Shelley-based era;
- the census, near-cap and collateral commands, with their plans and run times.

Results go to `preprod/<RESEARCH_SIM_TEST_TAG>/tests/` under the output root.
